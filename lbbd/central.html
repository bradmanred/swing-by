Central Park

Central Park in Dagenham is a beautiful place to relax and have fun. Whether you want to take a walk, play sports, try pitch and putt, or just enjoy nature, there is something for everyone. The park is loved by locals and visitors alike for its friendly atmosphere and variety of activities. 

Hidden amongst the vegetation you may notice pillboxes and other historical features dating back to the second world war.  

In 2014 a new footbridge was installed, which links Beam Valley to Bretons Park, opening up the valley for informal access on foot or bicycle. Look out for the Sustrans portrait bench with 3 life-size sculptures of historical and cultural figures from the area, chosen by the local community. 

Central Park was created in the 1930s alongside the Dagenham Civic Centre. It has grown over the years to include a children’s playground added in 1952 and the Memorial Garden opened in 2006. The park has been improved many times to make it a welcoming place for everyone. 

The Dagenham Town Show was an annual event held at Central Park from 1952 to 2011. It started as part of the Three Towns Show with Romford and Hornchurch but became a standalone event. The show featured horticultural displays, handicrafts, competitions, dog shows, parachute displays, fairground rides, fireworks, and more. It was a popular event but ended due to financial constraints. 

 Central Park has recently been upgraded with new trees and wildflowers, two new children’s play areas and nature zones. The tennis courts have been refurbished, and new pathways have been added to make it easier to explore the park. Additionally, extensive landscaping has created flowing hills and enhanced the park’s natural beauty  

Central Park is part of the Dagenham Corridor, which includes over 500 acres of green space. This corridor stretches from Hainault to the River Thames and includes Eastbrookend Country Park, The Chase Local Nature Reserve, Beam Valley Country Park, and Beam Parklands.

Do's

Be kind to wildlife – Enjoy watching animals and birds, but don’t disturb them.

Walking your dog? Keep them under control and always keep them on a lead near:

  • Playgrounds, play areas
  • Lakes (to protect nesting birds)
  • Public buildings
  • Always clean up after your dog using the bins provided

Picnics are welcome! Just remember to take all your rubbish home with you.

Countryside byelaws

The London Borough of Barking and Dagenham, like other councils, has wide powers to make byelaws in relation to a range of areas under its control. Generally, byelaws seek to uphold good order, government and public health

Countryside byelaws

Report to us

Please help the Ranger Service by reporting any problems you see or ideas that you have that will improve your enjoyment of the parks and nature reserves in the borough.

Report to us

Don'ts

Don’t harm or disturb wildlife – It’s against the law to hurt or scare wild animals or birds.

Don’t damage or disturb animal homes like nests or burrows, especially during breeding season.

No hunting or capturing. No traps, nets, or snares – These can harm protected animals.

Birds of prey can only be brought in with permission. Please see the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981.

No fishing, except at Eastbrookend Country Park (with a valid rod licence). Check fishing rules here.

No swimming in lakes or walking on them when they are frozen.

No model boats.

Fires, and barbecues are strictly forbidden.

Camping is not allowed.

No setting up tents, gazebos, or marquees without permission.

Don’t climb on:

  • Fences, walls, or railings
  • Trees or park structures (enclosures, posts…etc)
  • No motorbikes in the park
  • No model aircrafts or drones
  • No selling or trading in the park
