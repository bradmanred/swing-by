Streets for All Ramsbottom - town centre</h1></div></div></div>
Main content area</h2>
 </div>
We want to make it easier, safer and more attractive for everyone to travel to and around Ramsbottom town centre.</p>
Our aim is to:</p>
Improve connectivity to local places and services</li>
Provide safe and convenient travel options for everyone</li>
Create high quality public spaces</li>
These aims align with the vision set out in the Ramsbottom Town Centre Plan .</p></div></div>
The bigger picture</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 About Streets for All schemes<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Streets for All schemes are part of the multi-million-pound investment in the Bee Network that aims to transform the way we travel across Greater Manchester, with joined up services, affordable fares, and cleaner, greener transport.</p>
A key goal of the Bee Network is to ensure that by 2040, 50% of all trips – equivalent to one million daily journeys – are made by public transport, walking or cycling. Key benefits of more people regularly opting for sustainable travel will mean:</p>
Less road congestion.</li>
Improved air quality.</li>
Better health and wellbeing.</li>
A well-supported local economy.</li>
 </div></div></div>
 Funding for Streets for All schemes<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
This scheme is being funded by UK Government via the Streets for All element of Greater Manchester’s City Region Sustainable Transport Settlement, which is part of the investment in the Bee Network to transform the way we travel across Greater Manchester.</p>
This particular element of funding may only be spent on capital investment for walking, cycling, and sustainable transport improvements and cannot be used to fund other day-to-day revenue spend such as maintaining the roads.</p></div></div></div></div></div></div>
Revised proposals for Ramsbottom town centre</h2>
Following feedback received during public consultation in 2022 and 2024, we have developed revised proposals for enhancing connectivity and improving accessibility throughout Ramsbottom town centre and the surrounding areas. </p>
At the heart of the  proposals is a commitment to make travel easier and safer for everyone.  </p>
Initial proposals included introduction of a one-way system but due to significant concerns expressed during the 2024 consultation, this proposal has been removed from the revised scheme. </p>
You can find out more about the revised proposals below.</p>
You said, we did</h2>
During the consultation in 2024 we received 1504 responses. After considering the feedback we have revised the proposals. </p>
Below you can find out more about the proposals and some of the changes made after considering the feedback.</p></div></div>
 Open<!-- --> All </div>
 One-way system<!-- --> 
</path> </h2>
You said</h3>
You raised concerns about increased congestion, emergency vehicle access and the unsuitability of Kay Brow for HGVs.</li>
Local businesses highlighted potential impacts on footfall and delivery access.</li>
We did</h3>
The proposed one-way system has been removed from the scheme.</p></div></div></div>
 Widening and improving footpaths<!-- --> 
</path> </h2>
You said</h3>
Concerns were raised about widening pavements on Bridge Street to allow for the one-way system.</li>
There was strong support for widening and improving footpaths to enhance accessibility and safety.</li>
We did</h3>
Pavement widening along Bridge Street and at the northern end of Bolton Street near its junction with Bridge Street will no longer be implemented due to the removal of the one-way system. The pavement on Bridge Street between Crow Lane and Palatine Street has been widened to accommodate a shared use path for pedestrians and cyclists.</p></div></div></div>
 Improved pedestrian crossings<!-- --> 
</path> </h2>
You said</h3>
There was support for improved pedestrian crossings, particularly for enhancing safety and accessibility.</li>
Respondents also raised concerns about potential impacts on traffic flow and congestion.</li>
We did</h3>
We are still proposing a number of new and improved pedestrian crossings, though the location of these has changed following removal of the one-way system. In particular, we are proposing a new controlled crossing on Bolton Street, north of Kay Brow.</p></div></div></div>
 20-mile-per-hour speed limit<!-- --> 
</path> </h2>
You said</h3>
There was support for introducing a 20mph speed limit, as it improves safety for cyclists and pedestrians, with most comments highlighting its traffic-calming benefits about the speed limit.</p>
We did</h3>
The 20mph zone has been retained in the proposals.</p></div></div></div></div></div></div>
Next steps</h2>
Two presentation events were held at Ramsbottom Civic Hall on 28 May and 2 June 2026. Separate sessions were also held with local businesses.</p>
Following this engagement, Bury Council councillors will consider the revised scheme for approval. If approved, construction is expected to begin in 2027 and take approximately 12 months to complete.</p></div></div>
About the changes by area</h2>
You can download information boards and maps of these proposals and changes at the end of this page or read the descriptions below.</p>
Changes around Bridge Street and the market car park</h3>
These proposals and changes have been made following the consultation process around Bridge Street, the north end of Railway Street, Bolton Street, and the roads around the market car park.</p>
All of these streets will have a 20mph speed limit to help make travel safer for everyone.</p></div></div>
A description of what is being proposed and what has changed</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 What is being proposed?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
What is being proposed?</h3>
New raised table crossings on Bridge Street, Bolton Street, Railway Street, Square Street, Silver Street, Factory Street and Crow Lane.</li>
New signal-controlled pedestrian and cycle crossings at the junction of Bridge Street and Palatine Street and Railway Street at the junction with Union Street.</li>
New pedestrian and cycle crossing on Bridge Street at the junction with Palatine Street, and closure of Palatine Street to allow for the cycling route to Nuttall Park to be diverted from Railway Road. The pavement between Crow Lane and the new crossing has also been widened to accommodate shared use by pedestrians and cyclists.</li>
Union Street will be reconfigured to route traffic around the car park, with vehicle access restricted to residents, businesses, and delivery services. The section from the junction with Railway Street will be closed to general vehicle traffic, allowing access only for pedestrians and cyclists.</li>
Where possible, some road resurfacing, funded by the Council’s highways budget, will be incorporated into the scheme.</li>
 </div></div></div>
 What has been changed since the previous proposals?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The one‑way system planned for Ramsbottom town centre is no longer part of the scheme. Because of this change, the following proposed measures will also not happen.</p>
Installing a controlled pedestrian crossing at Bridge Street/Crow Lane (will be installed at Bridge Street/Palatine Street).</li>
Widening the pavements along Bridge Street and at the north end of Bolton Street near Bridge Street.</li>
Relocating the bus stop to Railway Street (will remain where it is).</li>
Junction improvements on Bolton Street/Bridge Street.</li>
 </div></div></div></div></div></div>
Changes around Kay Brow</h3>
These proposals and changes have been made following the consultation process around Kay Brow, the southern end of Bolton Street and Railway Street. Kay Brow will become part of the 20mph zone.</p></div></div>

Changes to Nuttall Park</h3>
These planned changes in and around Nuttall Park, are designed to make it easier to travel between the park and the town centre.</p></div></div>

 What is being proposed?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
We want to improve the paths through Nuttall Park so it’s easier to walk and cycle directly to and from the town centre. Improvements include installing dropped kerbs and continuous footpaths.</p>

Proposals aim to enhance connectivity, improve safety and accessibility, and deliver environmental benefits</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 Accessibility<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
By improving dropped kerbs and tactile paving at junctions we’re making Ramsbottom more accessible to everyone, including those with prams, mobility scooters and those with walking difficulties.</li>
Prioritising those walking and wheeling in more locations by adding controlled crossing facilities to busy roads, to help people move around the town centre.</li>
 </div></div></div>
 Connectivity<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Improving walking, wheeling and cycling options will allow more local residents to travel into and through the town centre more easily, giving more access to local facilities.</li>
By expanding and improving the walking, wheeling and cycling infrastructure, including high-quality crossings, we aim to enhance connectivity for everyone. This effort is focused on making Ramsbottom town centre more accessible and creating a seamless, enjoyable journey for pedestrians, wheelchair users and cyclists.</li>
 </div></div></div>
 Safety<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
We’re committed to making journeys smoother and safer for everyone -whether walking, wheeling, cycling, driving, or using public transport.</li>
By delivering new cycling infrastructure, improved pedestrian crossings, and targeted traffic‑calming measures, the proposals will create safer and more accessible journeys for all those travelling to and through Ramsbottom town centre.</li>
These improvements are designed to reduce conflicts between different road users, make crossing key routes easier and more intuitive, and encourage more people to choose active and sustainable travel.</li>
 </div></div></div>
 Health<!-- -->

Enabling active travel to encourage people to walk or cycle more has health benefits. Increasing physical activity contributes to healthier lifestyles.</li>

 Environment<!-- -->

What is a raised table?</h2>
A raised table is a section of the road that’s built higher than the surrounding surface, like a flat speed bump. It’s usually placed at pedestrian crossings or junctions to slow down vehicles and helps to make it easier and safer for pedestrians to cross the road.</p>
What is a controlled pedestrian crossing?</h2>
A controlled pedestrian crossing is a safer place to cross the road where traffic is stopped or directed by traffic lights or signs.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
