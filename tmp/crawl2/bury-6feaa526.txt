Dropped kerbs</h1></div></div></div>
Main content area</h2>
 Dropped kerb application form </div></div>
A dropped kerb, or vehicular access crossing, allows vehicles to cross the pavement from the road to a driveway.</p>
Without a vehicular access crossing, you may damage the kerb or utilities, such as gas or water mains that may be below the surface of the pavement. If you want a new dropped kerb, you must apply and be granted permission by Bury Council.</p>
Step 1: Things to consider before you apply</h2>
All requests for a dropped kerb are reviewed on an individual basis by a member of the highways team. However, here are a few things you can check before you apply:</p>
You must have a driveway (if you are thinking of creating a driveway within your front garden, we recommend you apply for a dropped kerb before you start any work).</li>
Your driveway must be a minimum length of 5 metres from the where your drive meets the pavement. However, there are circumstances where a greater length is required.</li>
Your driveway must be at least 3.1 metres wide.</li>
The gradient (or steepness of your drive) must be less than 10%.</li>
You must be the owner of the property (or have the written permission of the property owner).</li>
 Planning permission may also be required for a dropped kerb. We recommend you get this before you apply for a crossing. Planning permission will be required if:</p>
Your property is on a classified road</li>
Your property is in a conservation area .</li>
Your property is a listed building .</li>
The application is relating to access for a commercial or apartment building.</li>
 </div></div>
Please note</h2>
Bury Council considers it important to keep trees. If there is a highway tree in the pavement, the tree would not normally be removed to provide a dropped kerb.</p></div></div></div>
Step 2: Apply</h2>
Once you have checked you meet all the required criteria outlined in step 1, you can complete the dropped kerb application form .</p>
A non-returnable deposit of £85 is required.</li>
The fee covers the cost of administration and a site assessment by a member of our team.</li>
Following receipt of an application, we aim to complete assessments within 28 working days.</li>
Step 3: Approval</h2>
If we approve your application, we will contact you to advise how much the dropped kerb will be to install.</p>
The overall cost will vary depending on the size and complexity of the required crossing to suit your property.</p>
This type of work is now carried out in batches for efficiency purposes, and we aim to complete two batches of vehicular crossing works per year.</p>
We will advise of a commencement date at least 24 hours before the works are due to start.</p>
 Please note: </p>
Delays may still occur at short notice due to unforeseen circumstances.</li>
The non-returnable deposit will not be refunded if you decide not to go ahead with the work.</li>

Dropped kerbs - questions and answers</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 Who pays for installing a dropped kerb?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The applicant must pay a non-refundable administration fee to apply for a dropped kerb and we cannot guarantee your application will be successful.</p>
If your application is approved, you should then contact us for a quotation for the work.</p></div></div></div>
 Do I need planning consent?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Yes, if the dropped kerb is on a classified road (a main road). We will tell you if you need planning permission. If planning permission is required and you have not been sent a planning application form, please contact us.</p></div></div></div>
 Do I need a Legal Land Easement?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Yes, if the dropped kerb crosses over land which is not dedicated highway. We will tell you if you need a Legal Land Easement.</p></div></div></div>
 Can I carry out the work myself?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
No, only Bury Council are permitted to undertake this type of work on the adopted highway.</p></div></div></div>
 What may prevent me from having a dropped kerb?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
You may not be able to have a dropped kerb if:</p>
Your property is on a bend or at a road junction</li>
A tree is in the proposed crossing</li>
Your property is close to traffic signals</li>
There is a steep slope between your garden and the road</li>
There is insufficient garden area</li>
There is insufficient visibility.</li>
 </div></div></div>
 Does my garden need to be a certain length?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Yes, your garden must be a minimum of 5 metres in length.</p></div></div></div>
 What is the maximum size of a dropped crossing?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The maximum size of a dropped crossing is 4.5 metres. This is the equivalent of 5 dropped kerbstones outside one property.</p></div></div></div>
 When should I make a driveway in my garden?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
As not all applications are successful, we recommend that constructing a driveway is left until after a dropped kerb application is approved.</p></div></div></div>
 What if a tree on the pavement is affected by the dropped kerb?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Trees are important for our natural environment and cleaning the air. A tree will not normally be removed to provide a dropped kerb.</p></div></div></div>
 What about the water, gas pipes and cables under the pavement?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
If these are affected by the proposed dropped kerb, the cost for lowering or moving apparatus must be met by the applicant.</p></div></div></div>
 How do I get a streetlight relocated?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
In most cases, streetlights can be relocated to allow driveways to be widened. However, as this is considered to be an improvement to a private property, all costs will have to be borne by the householder. On competition of a site inspection, a quotation will be given. Enquire about relocating a street light. </p></div></div></div>
 What if I do not own the property?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
You will require permission from the property owner to park a vehicle in the front garden. Your tenancy agreement or deeds should explain this.</p></div></div></div>
 Will I own the dropped kerb?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
No, your dropped kerb will be part of the public highway. When you have a dropped kerb built, you are paying for the right for vehicular access to your property only. After construction, we will be responsible for maintaining the dropped kerb. Highways reserve the right to alter your dropped kerb in the future to accommodate dropped kerbs to neighbouring properties or highway refurbishment works.</p></div></div></div>
 How wide will the dropped kerb be?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The standard width of a dropped kerb is 2.7 metres. If you would like the dropped kerb to be wider or narrower than this, please state the width on the application form. The maximum width of a new dropped kerb is 4.5 metres.</p></div></div></div></div></div></div>
