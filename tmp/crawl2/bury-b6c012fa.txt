Main content area</h2>

Highway resurfacing is a lengthy, costly treatment that restores a deteriorated road surface to a new condition, lasting up to 20 years</div></div></div></div>
Each year, we spend in the region of £3.5 million on highway resurfacing schemes within the Bury borough.</p>
We also spend around £1 million per year on preventative maintenance . Investing in preventative maintenance helps extend the life of existing road surfaces, preventing major deterioration and reducing the formation of potholes - prevention is better than cure. </p></div></div></div>

The resurfacing process</h2>
Road resurfacing is a technique used when the surface or other layers of a road have deteriorated to a point where they need to be replaced.</p>
The process involves:</p>
Planing-off all or some of the existing surface and underlying layers</li>
Adjusting or replacing manholes and gullies, if required</li>
Application of a bitumen spray coat</li>
Laying of a new surface on top of the spray coat (the surfacing will consist of either one or two separate layers, depending on the condition of the road underneath)</li>
The newly laid surface is then rolled to ensure it is smooth</li>
We can carry out a full road resurfacing scheme at any time of year and the length of time required to carry out the resurfacing work depends on the depth required to work down to and the quantity of material that requires replacing.</p>
Resurfacing restores the road surface to a new condition, removing surface problems and restoring skid resistance. This is an expensive treatment that often requires extensive road closures. Resurfacing has a lifespan of up to 20 years.</p></div></div>
