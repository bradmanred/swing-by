Milltown Street Bridge, Radcliffe - £3.2m bridge reinstatement project</h1></div></div></div>
Main content area</h2>
Background information</h2>
The former Milltown Street Bridge was destroyed in 2015 during the Boxing Day floods.</p>
This £3.2 million bridge reinstatement project restores a long missing link in Radcliffe’s local infrastructure and significantly improves walking and cycling access to and from Radcliffe town centre and neighbouring communities.</p>
Bridge design</h2>
The replacement bridge sits higher above the river than the original to improve flood resilience. Its single span design eliminates the need for in-river supports, reducing the risk of future damage and simplifying construction.</p></div></div>

Scope of the bridge reinstatement project</h2>
The works included:</p>
Construction of a new 50m single span bridge</li>
Assessment of the existing reinforced concrete abutments (the structures that support the bridge at each end)</li>
Removal of what remains of the former bridge</li>
Removal of the existing pier (the structure in the river that previously supported the damaged bridge)</li>
Works to associated ramps and footways</li>
Environmental restoration such as managing invasive plants, protecting local wildlife, and improving the riverbank and greenspace habitats.</li>
The new bridge was fabricated in three sections and was transported to site by HGV. To install the bridge, a crane lifted the sections into position, bolted together and then welded.</p>

Funding</h2>
The cost to replace this 50m long bridge was £3.2 million. This cost reflects the bespoke design and the complex, high risk nature of the works and challenging site conditions.</p>
The bridge reinstatement project was funded by UK Government’s City Region Sustainable Transport Settlement (CRSTS) and the Mayor’s Challenge Fund.</p></div></div>
Extent of the works</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 Removal of existing structure and river pier<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The former bridge and its pier located within the river channel have been fully removed. This required specialist in-river demolition and environmental protection measures.</p></div></div></div>
 Environment Agency permissions<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The team worked closely with the Environment Agency to obtain the necessary consents and permits to undertake works in the river, ensuring flood risk and ecological impact were carefully managed.</p></div></div></div>
 Tight site constraints (Pioneer Mill side)<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The working area was severely restricted, requiring a custom-designed access ramp to fit the available space. This impacted both design complexity and construction logistics.</p></div></div></div>
 Underground obstructions<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Large buried shipping containers were identified during the design stage and were removed as part of the planned works.</p></div></div></div>
 Building demolition and utility disconnections<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
A single building adjacent to the bridge was demolished, and all associated utilities (power, water, gas, telecoms) disconnected or diverted.</p></div></div></div>
 High-voltage electrical cables<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
High-voltage infrastructure running alongside the site limited available working space and required strict safety management during construction.</p></div></div></div>
 Invasive species management<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The presence of Japanese knotweed and hogweed required treatment by licensed specialists and ongoing monitoring in line with environmental legislation.</p></div></div></div></div></div></div>
General factors that make bridges expensive to build</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 Specialist materials and fabrication<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The bridge is a bespoke single-span weathering steel structure, built to last with minimal maintenance.</p></div></div></div>
 Complex engineering and construction techniques<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Installation required off-site and on-site fabrication, craning, structural precision, and careful sequencing in a constrained urban environment.</p></div></div></div>
 Compliance with design and safety standards<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The scheme meets full national design codes, including Disability Discrimination Act (DDA) accessibility, drainage, lighting, and fall protection. </p></div></div></div></div></div></div>
Your questions answered – Milltown Street Bridge and Pioneer Mill</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 Is safety a priority?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Yes. Keeping residents, businesses and visitors safe is a top priority. We understand the concerns raised and want to reassure people that safety has been central to planning and decision‑making.</p></div></div></div>
 Why has progress at Pioneer Mill been slow?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Pioneer Mill is a very complex site. Challenges such as multiple owners, previous fire and structural damage and the need for legal and regulatory action mean progress can take time. We recognise this can be frustrating for residents.</p></div></div></div>
 Is Bury Council taking action?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
We are coordinating a multi agency response including working closely with those responsible for the site. There have been recent positive steps towards improving the management and longer‑term future of the site. While issues won’t be resolved overnight, progress is being made.</p></div></div></div>
 Who is involved in managing safety?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
We are working with several agencies, prioritising public safety including Greater Manchester Police, Greater Manchester Fire and Rescue Service, the Environment Agency and the Health and Safety Executive. This joint approach helps improve monitoring, enforcement, and coordination.</p></div></div></div>
 What safety measures are there for when the bridge opens on 12 May 2026? <!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The following measures are in place or being completed:</p>
Bollards at both ends of the bridge to prevent vehicle access</li>
New fencing to restrict access beneath the bridge (to be completed before the end of May)</li>
CCTV cameras on both sides of the bridge, monitored through Bury Council’s network.</li>
 </div></div></div>
 Will Bury Council continue to keep residents informed?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Yes. We remain committed to working with the community and partners and to continuing dialogue to address safety concerns and improve conditions at the Pioneer Mill site.</p></div></div></div></div></div></div>
