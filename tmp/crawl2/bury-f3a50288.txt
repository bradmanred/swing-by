Fishpool and Pimhole Bee Active Travel Scheme (phase 2)</h1></div></div></div>
Main content area</h2>
This active travel scheme has been designed together with the community, to provide the best quality scheme for residents.</p>
There are two phases to the scheme. Phase one is complete and phase two started in November 2025.</p>
The scheme will improve connectivity to Bury town centre and other local facilities, making it safer and easier for people to walk, wheel or cycle for local journeys.</p>
This will allow people to reduce car trips and choose healthier, greener ways to travel. </p>
Scheme duration</h2>
The scheme will take approximately 13 months to complete, subject to weather conditions and other operational issues.</p>
Scheme start: Autumn 2025</li>
Scheme end: Winter 2026</li>
Project funding</h2>
As part of the Bee Network, this £5.7 million scheme is funded from UK Government’s City Region Sustainable Transport Settlement (CRSTS) and the Mayor’s Challenge Fund.</p></div></div>
What to expect during the works</h2>
As the scheme involves working in the road, there will be some unavoidable temporary disruption, such as:</p>
Alternative pedestrian routes and crossings</li>
Temporary traffic lights due to lane closures</li>
Diversion routes due to essential road closures</li>
Working hours</h3>
Generally, 9am to 5pm, Monday to Friday with any work outside of these hours only if needed</li>
Noise</h3>
Any noisy works won’t start before 8am</li>
 </div></div>
Work programme </h2>
The work programme is split into 12 individual projects, each focussed on specific roads and streets within the Fishpool and Pimhole areas.</p></div></div>
Thank you for your patience while works are in progress</h3>
To keep the community informed about when work will begin at each of the project areas, advance warning signs will be placed at the roadside shortly before work is scheduled to start. We will also share updates on our social media channels. </p></div></div></div>

The scheme design</h2>
The scheme design consists of a wide range of features that will improve safety and encourage people to walk, wheel or cycle. Work will involve:</p>
Upgrading existing junctions </li>
Creating new crossing points</li>
Introducing traffic calming measures </li>
 </div></div>
New signal-controlled pedestrian and cyclist crossings at Heywood Street/Parker Street</div></div>
New signal-controlled pedestrian crossings at the Manchester Road/Parkhills Road junction</div></div></div></div></div></div></div></div>
The new features explained</h2>
Here is a description of each of the new features and where they will be located.</p></div></div>

 Upgraded junctions<!-- --> 
</path> </h2>
Junction design improvements vary per junction, but include:</p>
widening footpaths and pedestrian waiting areas</li>
controlled pedestrian crossings (where pedestrians are given priority with traffic signals to cross the road)</li>
making turns sharper at road junctions (slowing vehicle turning movements to improve pedestrian and cyclist safety at road junctions) </li>
raised road tables</li>
cycle facilities</li>

Questions and answers</h2> Open<!-- --> All </div>
 How will I know when work is due to start?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
To keep the community informed about when work will begin at each of the project areas, advance warning signs will be placed at the roadside shortly before work is scheduled to start. </p></div></div></div>
 How is the scheme funded?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Funding for active travel schemes comes from a range of sources, including the UK Government’s City Region Sustainable Transport Settlement (CRSTS), which saw Greater Manchester awarded £1.07bn to help deliver the Bee Network.</p>
 Find out more about active travel schemes and the Bee Network </li>
 </div></div></div>
 Will access be maintained?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
We will try to maintain vehicular access to all businesses and properties during the works, but access may be limited when we are removing or replacing the footway and, or road surface.</p></div></div></div>
 What about access for emergency vehicles?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
All the emergency services will be notified well in advance of the commencement of the works and will be made aware of any restrictions in place.</p>
Should emergency vehicles require emergency access to residential or business properties affected by the works, the contractor shall direct and, or escort them through the works.</p></div></div></div>
 Who will control the works?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Our Engineering Services Team will co-ordinate and supervise the works from start to finish.</p></div></div></div>
 Safety of works<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
The contractors we employ are responsible for ensuring works are carried out safely. If you notice anyone tampering with or taking down signs or barriers, especially at night, please call the emergency number provided. Your call could prevent an accident.</p></div></div></div>
 Be aware<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
Criminals have been known to use works on the highway as an excuse to get into nearby properties to commit a crime.</p>
If you are approached by anyone claiming to be from Bury Council or a utility company, always ask for ID.</p>
If you are unsure, don't let them in and call the police.</p></div></div></div>
 Who do I contact for any enquiries?<!-- --> 
</path> </h3>
If you experience any issues during the construction works then please contact us.</p>
Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm: Phone Customer Contact Team on 0161 253 5353.</p>
Email: engcon@bury.gov.uk </p>
In an emergency out of the above hours call: 0161 253 6606.</p></div></div></div></div></div></div>
