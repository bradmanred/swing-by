Middleton Top Countryside Centre</h1>
The Middleton Top Countryside Centre is situated on the High Peak Trail, Middleton by Wirksworth.</p>
Share this page</li>
 Share via Facebook </li>
 Share via Twitter </li>
 Share via LinkedIn </li>

 </div>
 Barbecues are not permitted at any of our sites.</p>
Middleton Top, as you might expect, is at the top of a hill and offers delightful views of the surrounding countryside.</p>
This site has a visitor centre, gift shop and cycle hire centre , car park, toilets and picnic area. Whether on foot, horse or bike it is an ideal place to start your visit along the beautiful High Peak Trail .</p>
It houses the restored steam engine house built in 1829 by the Butterley Company to haul wagons up the Middleton incline.</p>
Middleton Top Engine House is currently closed to visitors due to essential unforeseen maintenance works. We hope to welcome visitors back in 2027. Reopening dates will be published on this page and on our social media channels as soon as they're confirmed.</p>
The countryside centre sells a range of books, maps, leaflets and gifts, along with hot and cold drinks, ice creams and snacks.</p>
There are plenty of places nearby to enjoy a scenic picnic including Redhill Quarry Nature Reserve located behind Middleton Top. Here you'll find wildflowers growing in abundance in the summer, including several species of orchid.</p>
Visitor opening hours</h2>
Visitor centre summer opening hours</h3>
April to August from 9.30am to 4.45pm.</p>
September to October: weekends only and October half term.</p>
Toilets are open 9am to 5pm. The accessible toilet requires a RADAR key which can be borrowed from the visitor centre during opening hours.</p>
Visitor centre winter opening hours</h3>
Weekends only, November to January from 9am to 3.45pm.</p>
Weekends only, February to March from 9.30am to 4.15pm.</p>
Toilets open 9am to 4pm. The accessible toilet requires a RADAR key which can be borrowed from the visitor centre during opening hours.</p>

Cycling routes</h2>
Middleton Top is a great base to start your bike adventure. Here are a few suggested routes.</p>
Carsington Cracker</h3>
The route heads down to Carsington Water and follows the national cycle network to the pretty village of Tissington. Here it joins the Tissington Trail and heads northwards past the old signal box at Hartington.</p>
Just before the visitor centre at Parsley Hay the route re-joins the High Peak Trail and returns to the start at Middleton Top.</p>
Follow the Carsington Cracker route on Komoot .</p>
Minninglow Meander</h3>
The route follows the High Peak Trail past the ancient burial site at Minninglow. From there it heads over Carlemere Lane to Biggin and joins the Tissington Trail for a short while.</p>
Then it's down the appropriately named Green Lane towards Middleton by Wirksworth before heading back up to Elton.</p>
You then follow a combination of quiet country lanes before re-joining the High Peak Trail and heading back to the start.</p>
Follow the Mininglow Meander route on Komoot .</p>
Derwent Dipper</h3>
The Derwent Dipper starts off along the High Peak trail going past the limestone outcrop called Harboro Rocks.</p>
The route then passes over the roof of the Peak following country lanes to the village of Elton. From here you pass though Birchover and close to the Nine Ladies stone circle at Stanton in Peak.</p>
The route drops steeply down to the valley floor at Rowsley and joins the White Peak Link. If you're lucky you may see one of Peak Rail's steam trains which run close to the trail in the summer months.</p>
At the end of the trail you go through Matlock and on to Starkholmes. At Cromford follow the canal towpath to High Peak junction before heading up the incline back to the start.</p>
Follow the Derwent Dipper route on Komoot .</p>
Hartington Quarter</h3>
The route heads up the High Peak Trail towards the Neolithic burial site at Minninglow.</p>
It then heads of down National Cycle Network (NCN) route 548 to the pretty village of Hartington which is famous for its cheese. From here you continue following  the NCN along a gated road though the hamlet of Pilsbury before climbing up and re-joining the High Peak Trail just after Parsley Hay.</p>
The route then heads south following the trail back to the start.</p>
Follow the  Hartington Quarter route on Komoot .</p>
Rail Trail</h3>
The Rail Trail uses a combination of some of Derbyshire’s disused railways that have been converted into multi-use trails.</p>
From Middleton Top the route follows the High Peak Trail its end at Dowlow.</p>
It then goes along the Pennine Bridleway along a combination of white roads and quiet lanes to Blackwell. From here it drops steeply downhill before joining the Monsal Trail at Millers Dale.</p>
The route meanders through the Wye Valley through a series of tunnels culminating in crossing the river at the Monsal Viaduct.</p>
At the end of the Monsal Trail at Bakewell the route heads up Coombes Dale before joining the White Peak Link at Rowsley.</p>
Once you've reached the finish of the White Peak Link the Rail Trail heads through Matlock and climbs up through Starkholmes before reaching the Cromford Canal.</p>
After following the canal towpath to High Peak Junction the route returns to the start via the High Peak Trail.</p>
Follow the Rail Trail route on Komoot .</p>
