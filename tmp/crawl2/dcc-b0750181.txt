Countryside volunteering</h1>
As a countryside volunteer you can get involved with all manner of countryside management work, ranging from wildlife conservation to visitor care. It's a great way to meet new people, learn new skills and keep fit. And it's fun too!</p>

People volunteer for different reasons. Some want to improve their career prospects, whilst others want something fulfilling to do after retirement. Many just want to give something back to society. Whatever the reasons, we really appreciate the hard work and dedication of all our volunteers.</p>
We have a range of volunteering roles. Opportunities vary depending on the nature and operation of the site, and the places available.</p>
Opportunities range from conservation and estate management work through to visitor services and education. You can also get involved in surveying and maintaining our Greenways and Public Rights of Way network.</p>
Our volunteer agreement and guide to volunteering with our countryside service give more information on procedures for volunteers and will help you decide if volunteering with us is right for you.</p>
Interested? Check out the following roles:</p>
 High Peak Junction railway workshops volunteer </li>
 wildlife garden volunteer </li>
 estate / conservation management team volunteer </li>
 ranger / warden support volunteer </li>
 visitor services volunteer </li>
Or download the volunteer enquiry form, complete it, return it to us, and we'll let you know what's available.</p>
Our countryside volunteers</h2>
Robin Freeman had been a visitor at Shipley Country Park for 30 years before he decided to give something back and become a volunteer.</p>
He's been helping out at the park for 2 years now.</p>
 "We get involved in all sorts of things - fencing, hedge laying, planting, path repairs. It's very different from the engineering job I used to do but it's great to be able to get outside and learn new skills. They're a great bunch of people, we work hard but we also have a lot of fun." 
Phil Rhodes volunteers at Pleasley Pit Country Park with their nature study group. He says:</p>
 "I went for a walk around the site and got talking to some of the volunteers and decided to get involved myself. I work on monitoring small animals like mice, shrews and voles and weigh, measure and log them. I have a busy day job so it's a release for me - I really look forward to my Saturday mornings outdoors." 
Working with community groups</h2>
We also work with volunteer groups such as 'friends' groups, Ramblers and parish path groups to maintain and improve our countryside sites and Public Rights of Way. Get in touch if your group wants to get involved, or if you want to start a new group.</p>
Elvaston Castle Secret Garden</h2>
 Elvaston's Secret Garden project involves volunteers in growing fruit and vegetables and includes a heritage allotment. It also provides opportunities for adults with learning disabilities and currently supports 2 groups.</p>
Local access forums</h2>
Derbyshire's local access forums have an advisory role in strategic access and recreation issues. They are made up of volunteers representing landowners, forestry and farming, countryside users, and local communities.</p>
There are vacancies on the Derby and Derbyshire Local Access Forum for anyone who is interested in helping to improve and promote the fantastic outdoor recreation opportunities which the area has to offer.</p>
