Definitive map and statement</h1>
The definitive map and statement is the legal record of public paths and provides conclusive evidence of their existence and status.</p>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
The definitive map shows the location of public paths and the statement records their status, description, width, length, structures and surface.</p>
Public right of way map</h2>
You can view the Leeds public right of way map online .</p>
Definitive paths are shown in black. These are routes that the public have a proven legal right to use.</li>
Permissive paths are shown in pink. These are routes that the landowner has granted the public permission to use.</li>
Recreational routes are shown in yellow. These are promoted routes which use a mix of definitive public rights of way, permissive paths, adopted highways and may also have sections running over private land.</li>
Disclaimer: This is a working copy of the definitive map and is not a legal document. There is no guarantee of accuracy and it should not be used to determine the location or alignment of paths.</p>
To view the definitive map and statement, registers, or further information contact the Public Rights of Way service at prow@leeds.gov.uk .</p>
</div>
</div>
Public right of way claimed routes map</h2>
 View the Leeds PROW claimed routes map .</p>
This map shows the location of claimed paths in Leeds. These routes do not carry a definitive public right of way, meaning the public does not have a proven right to use these paths.</p>
Some claimed paths may be available for use, but many are not. As claimed paths are not recorded on the definitive map, Leeds City Council does not maintain or enforce these routes.</p>
Changing the definitive map and statement</h2>
Unrecorded paths can be added to the map by a Definitive Map Modification Order (DMMO). Anyone can apply for an order but it must be supported by documentary evidence or evidence of use without interruption for 20 years.</p>
Public paths can be diverted, created, or extinguished by Public Path Orders (PPO). If affected by development, orders are made under the Town and Country Planning Act 1990. Under the Highways Act 1980, paths can be diverted to benefit the landowner, created where needed, or extinguished if not needed.</p>
Public Rights of Way can only be extinguished by a legal order or by direction of the Court.</p>
DMMO - Investigating a new public right of way</h2>
It is possible for public rights of way to be added to the Definitive Map and Statement if evidence is available to reasonably allege that a right of way exists. Members of the public can submit an application for a DMMO to add a route to the definitive map, under the provisions of the 1981 Wildlife and Countryside Act.</p>
In the first instance, contact PROW@leeds.gov.uk with a map showing the route you wish to claim. This will allow us to check if the route is already a public right of way or subject to an existing DMMO application. Once this has been ascertained, we can provide further guidance as to the process to submit an application and provide the relevant application forms. An application must be supported by evidence (either user evidence or documentary evidence), which the applicant must collate. The applicant must also serve notice on all affected landowners and occupiers.</p>
When the application is investigated, all relevant available evidence (whether for, or against the existence of a public right of way) will need to be considered to determine whether or not there is sufficient evidence to reasonably allege that a public right of way subsists. Evidence from both historic and user sources is required to be considered and this takes time. We have a significant backlog of applications which are broadly investigated in chronological order and it will likely be a number of years before an application is investigated.</p>

Statutory declarations</h2>
Landowners can deposit a statement and map under section 31(6) of the Highways Act 1980 indicating any rights of way over their land and then make a make a statutory declaration to the effect that no additional rights of way exist.</p>
You can read the Register of Statutory Declarations and Town and Village Green Landowner Statements or alternatively, visit our offices to view the paper copy.</p>
Rights of Way Improvement Plan</h2>
The Rights of Way Improvement Plan forms a ten year management plan setting out areas for consideration with regard to how the council proposes to develop and improve the public rights of way network in Leeds for walkers, horse riders, pedal cyclists and those with visual or mobility impairments.</p>
The Rights of Way Improvement Plan was published in 2009, in accordance with the requirements of the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000, and is currently under review. It contains both broad Statements of Action and an Action Plan to guide service delivery in this respect.</p>
If you would like a copy of the plan by email, contact the Public Rights of Way team on PROW@leeds.gov.uk .</p>
