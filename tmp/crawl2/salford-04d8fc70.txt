EV pavement channels – safe home charging for residents without driveways</h1>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
 Many Salford residents do not have access to off‑street parking, which makes charging an electric vehicle at home difficult. Salford City Council is introducing a pilot scheme to install EV pavement channels as a safer way for residents to run a charging cable from their property to their vehicle. </p>
An EV pavement channel is a narrow, flush‑mounted unit installed in the pavement. It allows your charging cable to be placed safely within an enclosed slot, reducing the chance of it becoming a trip hazard or causing an obstruction on the footway.</p>
This page explains what EV channels are, who can apply, and how the scheme works. If your location is not suitable for a channel, our existing guidance on using a cable protector remains available as a fallback option.</p>
When considering adding a proprietary charging point to their home, residents need to consider current planning regulations. Further guidance is available on planning permission for electric vehicle charging points . Charging boxes and other charging devices are not permitted to overhang or encroach onto the pavement or highway, only the cable is allowed.</p>
You can contact the local planning authority for further information if needed. However, if you want a formal decision as to whether planning permission is required you will need to apply for a Lawful Development Certificate for which a fee is payable. Full details can be found on our submitting an application page .</p>
Why is Salford offering pavement channels?</h2>
The national guidance for local authorities encourages safe cross‑pavement solutions for people who cannot install a driveway. Channels help to:</p>
reduce the hazards caused by trailing cables across the footway</li>
support residents who cannot charge on private property</li>
improve safety and accessibility for pedestrians, prams and wheelchairs</li>
reduce reliance on cable protector mats, which can obstruct narrow streets</li>
support Salford’s wider EV strategy and climate objectives</li>
Channels are not appropriate everywhere, so applications are assessed individually by our Highways Licensing Team.</p>
Who can apply for a pavement channel?</h2>
You may be eligible if:</p>
you live in a residential property</li>
you do not have off‑street parking and cannot create it through planning</li>
a vehicle can normally be parked outside your home</li>
the footway outside your property is suitable for installation</li>
you have, or intend to install, a BS 7671‑compliant home chargepoint</li>
Applications will be assessed against Salford City Council’s Location Assessment Criteria, which consider safety, visibility, electrical clearances and the characteristics of your street.</p>
Will a channel reserve a parking space for me?</h2>
No. The installation of an EV pavement channel does not give any right to a parking space. Parking on the public highway remains first‑come, first‑served. You may only use the channel when parked legally and safely adjacent to it.</p>
How much does installation cost?</h2>
The council currently has access to a limited external grant for this pilot.</p>
This means:</p>
early applicants may have their installation costs and licence fee fully funded, subject to eligibility and remaining funding;</li>
once funding is allocated, future applicants may be asked to contribute to the installation cost.</li>
You will always receive a quotation before proceeding if a contribution is required.</p>
What conditions will apply if my application is approved?</h2>
If your location is approved, you will need to sign a Section 178 Highways Act licence, which includes conditions such as:</p>
using a single continuous charging cable (no adapters or daisy‑chains)</li>
keeping the channel lid fully closed when not inserting or removing the cable</li>
removing the cable immediately after charging</li>
parking legally and adjacent to the channel</li>
maintaining safe cable positioning at all times</li>
keeping appropriate public liability insurance</li>
reporting any channel defects immediately</li>
The licence does not transfer if you move home.</p>
How do I apply?</h2>
To apply, email: EVPavementChannels@salford.gov.uk  </p>
You will receive an automatic reply with the application form. Once submitted, our officers will complete an initial assessment and contact you about the next steps.</p>
What if my location is not suitable for a channel?</h2>
Where installation is not possible due to footway layout, highway safety or other constraints, you may still be able to charge your EV at home using a cable protector, provided it is used safely and in line with our existing guidance .</p>
Charging with a cable protector remains a fallback option only, and residents are responsible for ensuring the cable does not create a hazard. Unsafe cable arrangements may be removed under the Council’s existing Highways Act powers.</p>
More information</h2>
If you have a question before applying, please email: EVPavementChannels@salford.gov.uk  </p>
