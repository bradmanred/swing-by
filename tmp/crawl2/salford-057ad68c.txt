Executive summary
Intended to create a vision of the future for Peel Green Cemetery, this Management Plan
should be viewed as both a reference and a working document.
This Management Plan has been developed to compliment the values and priorities of
Salford City Council whilst providing a dignified and compassionate service that cares for the
needs of the bereaved.
Our values of Pride, Passion, People and Personal Responsibility clearly represent what the
council stands for and this is clearly demonstrated through the dedication and commitment
of the Bereavement Services Team.
This management plan was developed during 2022/23 by staff of Place Directorate working
closely with key partners and will be reviewed and revised on an annual basis.

facility within Salford and was intended that the reader would be given a comprehensive
insight into the cemetery from the history of the site from the 1870’s onwards right through to

1.0 Introduction
1.1 Plan summary
Salford City Council is responsible for the management and maintenance of four cemeteries in the city,
which include Agecroft, Peel Green, Swinton and Weaste plus two crematoria situated at Agecroft and Peel
Green.
During the average year the council undertakes over 2,000 cremations and 600 burials. Our prime aim is to
provide a high quality and dignified burial and cremation service for bereaved families of all different
religious and cultural backgrounds within the community of the city and surrounding areas.
The Peel Green Cemetery Management Plan has been produced to guide the management and
development of the cemetery. The management plan reflects the vision, aims and objectives over a five-
year period.
The aims and objectives are the method by which the previously mentioned “vision” will be realised and are
based on the key aims established by The Civic Trust for their Green Flag award.
These aims and their associated objectives will form the basis for the proposals and provide a foundation
for projects undertaken during the life of this plan. It will be revised annually to ensure that its management
objectives respond to changing circumstances and a work programme will be produced annually.
The Plan contains the following aims which provide direction for positive action at Peel Green Cemetery
over the period 2023 to 2028.
•   To provide a welcoming site
•   To ensure that the cemetery is healthy, safe & secure
•   To ensure that the cemetery is clean and well maintained
•   To ensure sustainable management of resources
•   To ensure appropriate management of conservation and heritage
•   To ensure effective marketing of the facility
•   Implement effective management strategies

1.2 Description of Peel Green Cemetery
Peel Green Cemetery covers 32 acres of land and was opened in 1879 as a cemetery ground with three
separate chapels, Catholic, Church of England and Non-Conformist.
In December 1955 the non-conformist chapel was converted into the crematorium and to date a total of
59,700 cremations have taken place.
Figure 5: The original entrance to Peel Green Cemetery
The original entrance was located on Liverpool Road and had to be relocated due to the development of
the motorway. There are more than 40 Commonwealth War Graves from the period of the Great War.
The Cemetery contains the original lodge which was the former office. This has now been sold and is in
private ownership. It is now a child therapy centre. The old burial chapel is currently being marketed for
leasing/renting and it is still currently maintained by Bereavement Services and Asset Management service.
It has been used for filming of a TV drama. All the funds raised through this venture was invested back into
the current Crematorium.
The Crematorium has public toilets adjoined to the building with disabled access. There is also a
floral tributes room which also gives the public access to the "Book of Remembrance". There is a mixture of
manicured lawns and to encourage the wildlife habitat, an area has been set aside for wildflower planting.
The cemetery contains small graves for ashes and there is ample space for new graves.
Gardens of Remembrance with a choice of memorials offer quiet contemplation. Designated parking areas
including Disabled parking bays which are located outside the Lodge and the Crematorium.

1.3.2 Legal Obligations
The provision of burial and cremation facilities are not a statutory function and therefore there is no
mandatory statutory duty to provide a cemetery, but once provided the Burial Authority (a Burial Authority is
a Local Authority established under the Public Health Act 1936), must keep the cemetery in good order and
repair. This includes all buildings, walls, and fences thereon and buildings provided for use therewith.
The main statutes governing its activities are The Local Authorities Cemeteries Order 1977 and the Local
Government Act 1972, which sets out the general power of management. Burial Authorities must:
•    Maintain a plan, numbering graves, and grave spaces
•    Maintain records of burials, using the numbering system in the plan
•    Keep a register of burials; the 1986 amendment Act allows computerised registers
•    Store the plan, records and register to keep them from loss or damage
•    Empowers Burial Authority to make regulations for the management of the cemeteries
•    Appointment of medical referee
In relation to cremation, The Cremation Acts 1902 & 1952 were introduced to impose restrictions and
guidelines to authorities to manage their crematoria in a similar way to the Burial legislation. From 1885
when the first crematorium was opened, up to 1902, there were eight crematoria in the country. In 1952,
the act was extended to allow provision for the Department for Constitutional Affairs to regulate the
cremation procedure. Crematoria are subject to emission legislation, and under the provisions of the
Environmental Protection Act, there must be a 50% reduction in the total amount of Mercury emitted to the
atmosphere by British Crematoria by December 2012.
The 1930 Cremation Regulations were introduced to ensure that the service is managed to a high standard
with the right calibre of staff to undertake this function and that each cremation is properly accounted for
administratively.
The power under section 2(1) of the local Government Act 2000 ensures that the local authority does not
do anything which they are unable to do by virtue of any prohibition, restriction or limitation on their powers
which is contained in any enactment (whenever passed or made).

1.6 History
Peel Green Cemetery was opened on 19th July 1879 and covers some 32 acres of land. To date over
44,883 interments have been carried out. The crematorium opened in May 1955 is also found within
the cemetery grounds. To date over 59,700 cremations have taken place.
“The New Cemetery for Eccles” read the headline in the Salford Weekly News of 7th June 1879. The article
ran “The opening of the new cemetery, which is now nearly completed is fixed for the middle of the current
month. The Roman Catholic portion will be consecrated by the Bishop of Salford on the 14th, the Church of
England portion by the Bishop of Manchester on the 16th and the non-conformists will hold an opening
ceremony on the 21st.
The site was purchased in the autumn of 1877 from the Bridgewater Trustees for a yearly rental of £300.
The ground is laid out to give 1,000 graves per acre and the apportionment is Church of England 12,000,
Dissenters 10,000, Roman Catholics 6,000 and the remaining 10,000 being at present “unappropriated”.
The site of the cemetery is on the verge of Chat Moss and is nearly two miles west of the Eccles Market
place”
Roman Catholic Ceremonial at the New Cemetery.
This was the headline in the Eccles and Patricroft Journal of 21st June 1879. It continues: “An event of
importance to members of the Roman Catholic persuasion took place on Saturday (14th June) afternoon
on the opening of the mortuary chapel and burial ground belonging to the Roman Catholics of the district.
The ceremony of blessing the chapel was performed by the Very Reverend Cannon Kershaw, Rural Dean
of the Salford Cathedral Districts, assisted by Rev Father Sharrock as Deacon”. The ceremony was
attended by a very large congregation, members of the Burial Board, the school children in processional
attire and officiating Priests. Afterwards, tea was taken at the Co-operative Hall in Eccles and Cannon
Kershaw took the opportunity to thank the Burial Board for providing such beautiful and commodious
grounds for the interment of the departed.
Consecration of the Church of England Portion.
Very large numbers of people congregated in the cemetery on Monday 16th June to witness the
consecration service conducted by the Bishop of Manchester and assisted by the Rev Cannon Pitcairn,
Rural Dean of Eccles. All the local Vicars and Curates and members of the Burial Board were in
attendance. The choir of St Mary’s Church, Eccles was accompanied by Mr W. H. Hall at the harmonium.
After the Bishop had delivered his religious address, he thanked the Burial Board for providing an
admirable burial ground, but regretted it was so far away from where most people lived, who could ill-afford
to convey their departed friends so far for interment. He also commented that it was such a pity that the
different religions had different potions and chapels and it was a painful reminder of the unhappy
differences that separated those who believed in one common Lord. After the ceremony, the officials
adjourned to the Bird-in-hand Hotel for a celebration dinner.
The “Designation” of the Non-conformist ground.
This took place on Saturday 21st June in the presence of three hundred spectators. The service was held
in the mortuary chapel (which is now the Crematorium). A procession was formed at the Registrar’s house
consisting of officers of the Board, headed by Mr Henry Leigh JP and the various church Ministers. They
proceeded to the mortuary chapel where the Rev. William. Place (Congregational Minister of Patricroft) led
the hymn “Before Jehovah’s awful throne” which was sung in a most hearty manner. Then he read a few
verses from the Psalms 90 and 103. Rev. R. Middleton (Wesleyan, Barton) led the next hymn “There is a
land of pure delight” and Rev. Ireland (Baptist, Eccles) read from the Bible. Prayers were offered by Rev.
James Harwood (Unitarian, Monton), and Rev. W. Ford (Wesleyan, Eccles), delivered the address. He
referred to the allocation of 10,000 graves, which was an awful number to contemplate. However, these
new sanitary conditions were a far cry from the over-crowded chapel yards. The service closed with a
prayer offered by Rev. J. Rendell (United Methodist Free Church, Patricroft). At the close of proceedings,
the clergy and the Board members adjourned to the Registrar’s house for a celebratory luncheon.
The first burial at Peel Green Cemetery.

Amelia Mills was the first person to be interred in the new cemetery. She died on 14th July 1879 aged 70
years and was buried in the Church of England portion on 19th July. She had lived at 518, Peel Green.
Strangely, the Eccles and Patricroft Journal did not report this event, but on the 16 July 1886, the
newspaper made reference to her interment, saying that since the burial of Amelia Mills, 1,200 bodies had
been interred at Peel Green. The Journal went on “a stroll through these well laid-out grounds will well
repay the visitor. Much progress has been made and on every side are manifest symptoms of industry. The
place looks fresh and pretty, and the hay having just been carted, the green award makes an effective
background to the shrubbery borders which line the principal walks leading from the entrance gates”. The
report went on to list and describe all the flowers and plants, “making a most brilliant display. The aim of the
Burial Board is not only to provide a fit resting place for the dead, but also a pleasant resort for the people”.
The cemetery cannot claim to be the last resting place of any famous people, but its precincts contain a
cross section of the local population from industry, commerce, local government, education, and sport.
A short list of luminaries buried within the cemetery is displayed at appendix 1.
2.0 Strategic context
2.1      Social and economic profile of the surrounding area
Peel Green Cemetery is the most westerly of Salford’s cemeteries (Figure 7) and largely serves
the Salford communities of Eccles, Worsley, Walkden, Irlam and Cadishead. These communities
range from the 1% to 10% on the index of deprivation. However, the nature of the site being a
cemetery and its proximity to motorway links can enable wider reach of visitors.

2.2      Salford City Council
Salford City Council’s mission statement is "to create the best possible quality of life for the people of
Salford”. To fulfil its mission, the council offers a wide range of local public services and facilities and
collaborates with other public service providers in Salford and Greater Manchester.
The council has recognised the strategic importance of high-quality parks and green spaces in its planning
framework and sees such assets as vital to achieving positive outcomes for residents.
2.2.1 City region
Salford is one of ten local authorities in Greater Manchester that make up the Association of Greater
Manchester Authorities (AGMA) and the Greater Manchester Combined Authority (GMCA).

2.5     Bereavement Services
The city council has a service pledge which ensures that burial and cremation facilities are provided in an
appropriate setting and in a dignified and correct manner.
The service is also committed to maintaining the cemetery buildings, ensuring the safety and welfare of all
people who work in and visit the cemeteries. In addition, it is continually looking at ways of improving the
standards of service
The following suggestions have been implemented which have led to improvements being made to the
service:
•     The main cemetery gates are opened at weekends to allow disabled and vehicular access.
•     New notice boards have been introduced to the cemeteries.
•     Improvements have been made in relation to specification and standard of grounds maintenance,
      including winter working programmes and the use of a mechanical road sweeper. A dedicated
      Cemeteries Ground Maintenance Team now look after the site.
•     New bins have been installed and have been placed on a regular collection schedule.
•     Plans to install more benches to the site after request from local community group.
•     Muslim Burial Chambers installed at both Peel Green and Agecroft Cemetery after consultation with
      the Muslim Community.
•     Special locks have been purchased for all cemetery gates, to prevent unauthorised access out of
      normal hours.
•     The flower beds within Peel Green cemetery have been replanted to improve the aesthetics, and to
      make the cemetery a more welcoming place for users.
•     The collection of surface water near the chapel has been resolved by constructing a drainage system
      to soak away the water.
•     Regular meetings take place with Bereavement Services Management and the Neighbourhood
      Management Team to discuss any issues which may arise.

3.7 Access
Spinal paths throughout the site are largely tarmac with some traditionally paved.
The Book of Remembrance room is located within the crematorium building along with a floral tribute area,
which allows for disabled access. The Book of Remembrance is also available online via the council’s
website.
There are public toilets adjacent to the Crematorium Chapel with disabled access and is open during
daylight hours.
A loop system has been installed at the crematorium to assist people with hearing difficulties to follow the
funeral service. A public address system has been installed to allow mourners who gather outside at large
funerals to participate in the service.
In response to a successful accessibility audit conducted at Agecroft Cemetery, arrangements are being
made for the Access All Areas disability group to assess Peel Green Cemetery for equal access of the site
and facilities. An action plan will be developed from any recommendations.
3.8      Friendly and approachable staff
There are two staff based at Peel Green Cemetery, the remaining staff are based at Agecroft Cemetery
and Turnpike Depot. There are a total of six cemetery operatives alongside a team of 7 grounds
maintenance operatives.

4.4 Headstone safety
In 2017 the Bereavement Service Manager reviewed and updated the Memorial Policy and Procedures in
line with The Institute of Cemeteries and Crematorium Management and following Guidelines Published by
the Ministry of Justice. Burial grounds: guidance on managing unstable gravestone. The policy on memorial
safety was based on guidance that has been developed by the Burial and Cemeteries Advisory Group in
England, which advises The Ministry of Justice on all aspects of burial law. The policy has been produced
following the publication “Managing the Safety of Burial Ground Memorials Practical advice for dealing with
Unstable Memorials” issue by the Ministry of Justice.
Over the past few years, the issue of memorial safety has from time to time been the subject of adverse
publicity and public distress – often because of over-zealous risk assessments or poor communication.
Considering this burial ground operators have expressed concern about how to respond appropriately to
the risks presented by unstable gravestones. Significant risks should of course be properly managed – but
the risk of injury from a gravestone or other memorial which has become loose and unstable is very low.
That is why the advisory group issued a joint letter to burial authorities in March 2007 to make clear that
any action to manage risks in burial grounds needed to be sensible, proportionate, and undertaken in a
sensitive way. This policy aims to take a sensible approach to meet Salford City Councils legal
responsibilities by following good practice to provide safe places to visit and work, and importantly make
sure that remedial work to reduce any risk of serious injury does not cause unnecessary distress to
bereaved families and others who value the great historical and environmental heritage of our cemeteries.
                                                      22
It is important to note that ownership of memorials remains with the family of the deceased. Where
memorials are found to require maintenance, every effort should be made to contact the family to effect
repairs. Only when the memorial poses a significant risk, such as imminent collapse in a way that could
lead to serious injury, does immediate action need to be taken to control the risk.
Salford City Council Bereavement Services recognises that it has a duty of care to ensure the safety of
employees, contractors and visitors who encounter memorials in council operated cemeteries
The policy considers historic problems faced by the authority caused by past poor installation methods and
ways of dealing with unstable, hazardous memorials that may pose a threat to safety.
Zone risk assessments are carried out in all cemeteries and closed church yards annually.
All individual memorials are inspected on a minimum 5 yearly cycle.
Safety inspections on memorials below 2.5 Meters are carried out by members of staff who have received
training in how to check the safety of memorials. Training was provided by qualified instructors from the
National Association of Memorial Masons and the Institute of Cemetery and Crematorium Management.
All memorials over a height of 2.5 meters are inspected by Structural Engineers. In March 2022
Bereavement Services commissioned Urban Vision to carry out Health and Safety inspection on all 4
Cemeteries under their control. This was completed in June 2017. Several memorials were found to be
unsafe and due to the age of the memorials it was not always possible to contact the grave owner.
Bereavement Services made the decision to repairs the memorials and to maintain the heritage and
historical value rather than to lay the memorials flat which could cause distress.

7.2 Biodiversity
Cemeteries by their very nature are quiet, undisturbed places for most of the time, this provides ideal
conditions for a wide variety of animal life. Trees, plants, birds, and mammals all flourish in the cemetery
environment and add their own special something that enriches the visitors’ experience.
Recent improvements include the erection of a natural fence line behind the recycling bays. The fence line
consists of Prunus Laurocerasus (Cherry laurel) which not only defines the area but will encourage wildlife.
The Ministry of Justice Community Payback scheme is active within the cemetery and have planted over
two-thousand daffodil and bluebell bulbs as well as aiding with woodland management.
The council has designated approximately 20,000m2 of land into a no-mow grassland management
scheme. This space is encouraged to grow naturally for grassland habitat and will received one cut per
year. A dead tree trunk has also been incorporated into the wildflower area which will provide a habitat for
insects.
Deadwood is encouraged in the woodland area, and where possible standing deadwood is retained.
Before Covid-19 a series of bird and bat box building, wildflower and bulb planting sessions, bug watching,
and bat walk events previously took place and were extremely successful.
7.3 Historical features
The Crematorium was a former old non-conformist burial chapel, which was converted into a crematorium
in May 1955. It was designed to preserve the sanctity and the architectural features of the old stone chapel,
whilst introducing the necessary features for its use as a crematorium. Since then, over 60,000 services
have taken place there. The chapel can seat up to 80 mourners.
There are 95 war graves at Peel Green Cemetery. A list of these individuals can be found on the
Commonwealth war graves commission website.
7.4 Arboriculture management

a few lesser seen trees in Peel Green Cemetery which are worthy of note as being relatively uncommon, of
a large size or good specimen such as Scots Pine (Pinus sylvestris) and Acer platanoides (Norway Maple).

Pro-active but sensitive marketing is vital to the future of Bereavement Services. The service has a
Marketing Plan available upon request.
The Bereavement Service marketing objectives outlined in this plan include:
•    Maintain existing share of cremation market in view of the increased competition from Radcliffe (194
     addresses are in direct comparison)
•    Maintain existing market share within the Salford burial market.
•    Identify potential opportunities to break into new and existing markets outside of the Salford
     boundaries.
•    Maintain and improve the services good reputation by retaining Customer Service Excellence &
     Charter for Bereaved accreditations.
•    Contribute to improving the environment by better control of emissions at all crematoria. Money
     allocated to purchase new emission monitoring equipment to obtain better control.
•    Improve service offerings by conducting market research into customer’s needs. The Operational
     Development team have carried out a survey where they visit all four cemetery sites and speak
     directly to members of the public [excluding those attending a funeral].
•    Adjust opening times to match customer’s requirements.
•    Maintain the current numbers of burials and cremations (2000 cremations per annum and 650 burials
     per annum.
•    Increase the number of cremation memorials sold by 5%.
•    To increase awareness of the variety of services that we offer amongst key target audiences.
•    To raise the profile of the Bereavement Services brand.
•    To continually promote the good work undertaken by Bereavement Services.
•    To communicate the services, we provide by continuing to produce high quality and consistent
     promotional materials.
•    To position bereavement services as a market leader.
•    Increase sales of memorials at all Cemetery sites by 5%.
Peel Green Cemetery serves the communities of Eccles, Worsley, Walkden, Irlam, Cadishead. Although
because of its proximity to the M60, there is a potential to attract visitors from further afield.
Information about cemetery can be found on the Peel Green Cemetery and Crematorium webpage on the
Salford City Council’s Website. Additionally the site notice board, community contacts and the Friends of
Salford Cemeteries newsletter are all utilised for sharing news and service updates.
Educational material can be found on the council’s Peel Green Cemetery webpage for schools and there is
an interpretation board for the wildflower meadow. Assessment of the appropriateness and feasibility of
increasing interpretation boards will be explored.

of having achieved the aims and objectives of the Management Plan. Feedback from judges will be
incorporated into the annual review of the management plan.

regularly carry out customer satisfaction surveys. The customer satisfaction surveys are carried by the
council's PLACE Directorate and are undertaken indicating the overall percentage of customers satisfied
with the service they have received. The surveys are used to gauge improvements which will lead to an
improved provision for local communities and park and cemetery users.
