Organising an event in a Salford public park, green space or open space</h1>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
Private events and use of parks and open spaces</h2>
Salford’s parks and open spaces are public places for everyone to enjoy and are not available for private hire or exclusive use.</p>
We’re unable to accept applications for events that restrict access to invited guests, specific groups, or memberships. This includes private parties, weddings, corporate functions, and invitation-only gatherings.</p>
All events held in our parks must be open and accessible to the wider public (either free or ticketed) and should not prevent others from using the space as normal. Organisers are expected to maintain general access wherever possible and ensure their event does not unfairly limit public use.</p>
In some cases, larger community events, festivals, or charitable activities may be considered where public access and rights of way are maintained. These are assessed on a case-by-case basis through the event application process.</p>
</div>
Event applications for Buile Hill Park – Autumn/Winter 2026</h2>
Buile Hill Park is home to a range of valued event spaces, including the newly refurbished Buile Hill Mansion, now hosting the Registrar Service for weddings and small ceremonies, and Buile Hill Park Hall, an established venue for community events.</p>
The park is entering an exciting new phase, with National Lottery Heritage Fund development funding secured to support community consultation and help shape future improvements.</p>
While this work progresses, event applications will be carefully managed to ensure the park remains a welcoming and inclusive space for the community. As a result, some new or larger event applications may be refused at this time.</p>
For further information, please contact eventapplications@salford.gov.uk .</p>
</div>
Why does Salford City Council require event paperwork?</h2>
Salford City Council requests complete and accurate event documentation to ensure that events are planned, managed, and delivered safely, legally, and responsibly.</p>
The information you provide enables the Council, its partners, and the Safety Advisory Group (SAG) to:</p> 
Identify and assess potential risks</p></li>
Confirm that appropriate control measures are in place</p></li>
Ensure compliance with relevant safety, licensing, and legal requirements</p></li> 
While submission deadlines may appear demanding, event documentation often requires review by several agencies and partner organisations. Sufficient time is therefore needed to assess the information provided, offer feedback, and allow any necessary amendments to be made before the event takes place.</p>
Your documentation also creates an important audit trail. This can be invaluable in the event of:</p> 
An accident or incident</p></li>
A complaint</p></li>
An insurance claim</p></li>
A formal investigation</p></li> 
Ultimately, our aim is to support you in meeting your legal responsibilities and to help ensure the safety of everyone involved in or affected by your event, including attendees, staff, volunteers, contractors, and the wider public.</p>

 Guide for event organisers </p>
 </div>
Tell us if you're planning to hold an event in Salford</h2>
If you’re planning to organise an event at a Salford parks, open or greenspace then you must notify us of the event .</p>
If your event is on private land, we would still like to you still notify us. </p>
You will need to complete an online single application form for all events taking place on council owned land.</p>
Events requiring an application include:</p> 
Community or not for profit events and gatherings</p></li>
Commercial events</p></li>
Charity or fund-raising events</p></li>
Fitness and coaching sessions</p></li>
Markets and car boots</p></li>
Marches and parades</p></li>
Sports events including road races and rides</p></li>
Events on or by water at Salford Quays</p></li>
Forest schools or environmental/educational activities</p></li>
Temporary installations or exhibitions </p></li> 
Applications for a season or series of events can be included on this one form.</p>
The form should be completed by the event organiser, and they must include the details of your organisation’s public liability insurance cover for your event.</p>
Some useful links and downloadable documents on planning for a safe events are available on this website. </p>
Applications to hold standalone funfairs, bouncy castle attractions and circuses</h3>
Please email eventapplications@salford.gov.uk with details of the venue, dates and description of your activities. You will need to provide risk assessment, insurance details valid ADIP and PIPA safety certificate. Fee and refundable bonds are required.</p>
Application for fitness session, regular runs and Forrest schools in parks </h3>
These require and application with documents related to your qualifications, insurance, DBS checks and training levels. Fee may apply.</p>
Applications for markets and car boot sales</h3>
You will be required to follow the guidance notes for applying for a market or car boot licence. Fees will apply. </p>
Food and beverage traders</h3>
Risk assessment, insurance policy number, value and date, food hygiene rating, which local authority they are registered with and confirmation of gas certificate if applicable are required from all your Food and Beverage Traders. </p>
Advertising your event on the adopted highway or park railings in Salford </h3>
Please note that under Section 132 of the Highway Act 1980, it is illegal to place or attach banners or posters etc to any street furniture on the adopted highway, for example pedestrian guardrail, lighting columns, traffic poles and so on. Any such advertising will be removed, and the appropriate cost recharged to the relevant event organiser. Should an event organiser have an existing refundable bond with the city council, the fee will automatically be deducted from that bond. </p>
Please get permission for the land owner before placing banners or posters advertising your event on their property.</p>
For permission for banners and posters to be displayed on park railings contact parks@salford.gov.uk  </p>
 You must apply to hold an event in Salford a minimum of six weeks before your event date. There are three steps involved in processing your event application, these are explained below. </p>

 Health and Safety Executive guidance on running an event safely </p>
 </div>
The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) is reminding organisers to protect event workers and the public by using their event safety webpages. </p>
Whether you’re organising a small gathering or large-scale event, HSE guidance provides practical advice to help you meet your health and safety obligations. </p>
HSE’s guidance helps to plan, manage and monitor events effectively with three key sections: </p> 
 Getting started with event planning – ensure planning is proportionate to your event’s scale and risk level  </p></li>
 Managing an event – understand legal duties as an event organiser  </p></li>
 Planning for incidents and emergencies – develop effective response plans appropriate to potential risks  </p></li> 
</p>
 </div>
 Counter terrorism planning for outdoor events in Salford </p>
 </div>
Outdoor events attract large numbers of people and rely on effective planning by the event organiser to ensure the safety of attendees, staff, volunteers and contractors. Counter terrorism awareness is an important part of event safety and should be considered alongside existing event management, emergency planning and security arrangements.</p>
The Salford Safer Advisory Group and GMP and can provide advice and support to help event organisers understand potential risks and identify proportionate security measures for their event.</p>
Why counter terrorism awareness matters</h2>
Whether organising a community festival, sporting event, market, parade or large-scale public gathering in Salford, being alert to potential threats can help prevent incidents and improve safety for everyone involved.</p>
Event organisers should ensure that personnel working at events understand:</p> 
How to identify suspicious behaviour and activity.</p></li>
The importance of vigilance in crowded public spaces.</p></li>
How and when to report concerns.</p></li>
Emergency response procedures.</p></li>
The principles of  Run, Hide, Tell in the event of an attack.</p></li> 
Good practice for outdoor events</h2>
Before the event</h3>
Organisers should:</p> 
Include terrorism considerations within event risk assessments.</p></li>
Ensure staff and volunteers complete relevant awareness training.</p></li>
Brief staff, stewards and contractors on reporting procedures.</p></li>
Review site access points, vehicle routes and security arrangements.</p></li>
Identify emergency evacuation and invacuation (lockdown) procedures.</p></li>
Establish clear communication arrangements with event staff and emergency services.</p></li>
Consider the implications of Martyn's Law and any applicable requirements.</p></li> 
During the event</h3>
Staff, stewards and volunteers should remain alert for:</p> 
Unattended bags, packages or suspicious items.</p></li>
Vehicles parked in unusual or prohibited locations.</p></li>
Individuals attempting to access restricted areas.</p></li>
Behaviour that appears unusual, hostile or out of place.</p></li>
Suspicious photography, filming or information gathering.</p></li>
Anyone conducting repeated site visits without a clear purpose.</p></li> 
If you have concerns</h3>
Follow the See, Check and Notify (SCaN) approach:</p>
 See 
Remain observant and identify anything unusual or suspicious.</p>
 Check 
Consider whether there may be a legitimate explanation.</p>
 Notify 
Report concerns immediately through event management procedures, security staff or the police as appropriate.</p>
If there is an immediate threat to life, call 999 .</p>

Key message for event organisers</h2>
Creating a safe and secure event environment is everyone's responsibility, however event organisers will be required to demonstrate that plans are included in documents provided to the council.</p>
By undertaking ACT Awareness training, promoting vigilance amongst staff and volunteers, and planning proportionate security measures, event organisers can help ensure outdoor events in Salford remain welcoming, safe and resilient.</p>
 </div>
 Event applications for events with food traders in Salford </p>
 </div>
Your event food and drink traders will be expected to provide up to date, readable paperwork files including:</p> 
Their FSH food rating with a minimum 3 rating</p></li>
Street trading consent or licence paperwork</p></li>
Evidence of public liability insurance</p></li>
Adequate hand washing facilities</p></li> 
For further details on hand washing facilities, please read the guidance at the bottom of this page. Food traders without hand washing facilities will not be allowed to trade at your event in Salford.</p>

 Guidance on hire of gazebos, marquees and temporary structures </p>
 </div>
Why choosing the right supplier matters</h2>
Temporary structures such as gazebos, marquees, tents, tipis, stretch tents and other demountable structures play a key role at many events. Selecting a competent supplier is essential to ensure the safety of staff, contractors, performers and attendees, as well as the overall success of your event.</p>
Price should not be the sole deciding factor. A reputable supplier will provide safe equipment, trained staff, appropriate documentation, contingency arrangements, and support throughout the event lifecycle.</p>
What is the structure being used for on site</h2>
 Shelter and weather protection for attendees, staff, performers, or equipment. </p>
 Stage and performance areas for entertainment, presentations, or ceremonies. </p>
 Commercial and catering facilities for traders, exhibitors, and food vendors. </p>
 Event operations and administration such as event control rooms, ticket offices, or staff welfare areas, medical support </p>
 Storage of equipment and supplies throughout the event. </p>
 Security and access control including entrance canopies, search areas, and checkpoint structures. </p>
 VIP, media, or hospitality areas to provide dedicated facilities for specific guests.</p>
Selecting a suitable contractor</h2>
When appointing a supplier, event organisers should consider:</p> 
Proven experience delivering similar events. </p></li>
Financial stability and a good trading history. </p></li>
Adequate staffing, equipment and transport resources. </p></li>
Availability of emergency and out-of-hours support. </p></li>
Evidence of staff training and competency. </p></li> 
Documentation to request</h2>
The supplier should be able to provide relevant documentation, including:</p> 
Public and Employers' Liability Insurance certificates.</p></li>
Risk Assessments and Method Statements.</p></li>
Safety Policy.</p></li>
Structural calculations where required.</p></li>
Fire-retardancy certification.</p></li>
Crew training records and competency evidence.</p></li>
Construction Phase Plan (where applicable under CDM).</p></li> 
Site information</h2>
Organisers should provide suppliers with sufficient site information, including:</p> 
Site plans and access arrangements.</p></li>
Details of underground and overhead services.</p></li>
Ground conditions and any known hazards.</p></li>
Restrictions affecting vehicle or plant access.</p></li>
Information on nearby buildings, trees and other structures. </p></li>
Where necessary, anchorage testing or ground assessments may be required to confirm the structure can be safely installed. </p></li> 
Health and safety responsibilities</h2>
For larger events, the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 (CDM) may apply. </p>
Safe erection, operation and dismantling require:</p> 
Competent contractors and trained crews.</p></li>
Suitable risk assessments and safe systems of work.</p></li>
Appropriate supervision.</p></li>
Formal handover and sign-off procedures. suppliers should provide evidence that the structure has been designed and installed in accordance with the manufacturer's requirements and appropriate structural standards, including wind loading calculations where relevant</p></li>
Where ballast is used instead of ground anchors, evidence should be provided that the ballast arrangement is sufficient for the anticipated loading and weather condition</p></li>
Where members of the public will occupy the structure, arrangements should be made for adequate means of escape, emergency lighting where necessary, and appropriate fire-fighting equipment.</p></li>
Where electrical installations are incorporated within a temporary structure, organisers should ensure that installation and testing is carried out by competent persons and appropriate certification is available</p></li>
Where temporary flooring, staging, viewing platforms or raised areas are installed, evidence should be provided that they are suitable for the intended loading and use</p></li>
Ongoing inspection and maintenance throughout the event</p></li> 
Weather management</h2>
Wind remains one of the most significant risks to temporary structures.</p>
Organisers should ensure that:</p> 
Weather conditions are monitored throughout the event.</p></li>
Wind management procedures are in place.</p></li>
Emergency actions and evacuation thresholds are clearly defined.</p></li>
Contractors can respond to adverse weather conditions if required. </p></li> 
Checklist of items</h2> 
Structure type, size and specification</p></li>
Installation and dismantling dates</p></li>
List of documentation requirements from supplier</p></li>
Insurance arrangements</p></li>
Emergency support arrangements</p></li>
Responsibilities of both parties</p></li>
Handover certificate</p></li>
Inspection regime during the event</p></li>
Maximum wind speed operating limits</p></li>
Emergency weather procedures</p></li>
Details of anchoring or ballast arrangements</p></li>
Contact details for competent person on-call during the event</p></li> 
Key questions to ask your supplier</h2> 
Can you provide references from similar events?</p></li>
What contingency arrangements do you have?</p></li>
What documentation can you provide?</p></li>
How do you manage severe weather?</p></li>
What emergency support is available during the event?</p></li>
Are your staff appropriately trained and competent?</p></li>
How will the structure be formally handed over as safe for use? </p></li> 
Final recommendation</h2>
Event organisers should undertake appropriate due diligence and select suppliers based on competence, safety, experience and support arrangements rather than lowest cost alone. </p>
Working with a reputable, well-documented and appropriately accredited supplier will significantly reduce risk and help ensure a safe and successful event. </p>
Salford City Council will require you to supply paperwork from your supplier, however it is important that you keep these documents on file with your organisation. This creates a clear audit trail and helps evidence that the event has been planned and managed safely and responsibly.</p>

 Pressure on North West Ambulance Service on event medical cover </p>
 </div>
Event organisers should read the guidance from North West Ambulance Service at the bottom of this page regarding medical cover for their event. Currently getting an ambulance to your location in the event of an emergency may take longer than expected.</p></li>
You must fully outline your first aid and emergency planning in your event management plan.</p></li>
First Aiders should be trained to a FREC level 3. Staff with ‘First Aid at Work’ certification should not be used as First Aiders at an outdoor events in Salford.</p></li>
Please ensure you have medical staff, facilities and equipment on site to keep your patient/s stable, warm and comfortable until help arrives.</p></li>
You can call 999 on a mobile phone and give accurate and clear details of the emergency and location, and be prepared to take advice over the phone from the call handler.</p></li>

 Animals at events on council managed land </p>
 </div>
Salford City Council take animal welfare at events very seriously. The council have strengthened the policy on live animals at events on council-owned land, including our parks and open spaces. Event organisers will not be permitted to use animals for entertainment purposes, such as performing animals, animals in costumes for amusement purposes including animal rides, at events on council land from March 2024.</p>
Permission on request will be given for dogs shows, Gymkhana and pony shows, animals exhibited for educational purposes and wild bird display proving animal welfare and licensing documents are in place and in date at the time of the event.</p>
For the full policy and guidance notes on animals at events, please download the document at the bottom  of this page. If you need further guidance email eventapplications@salford.gov.uk </p>
 </div>
 Insurance </p>
 </div>
Please ensure that you arrange adequate insurance cover for the event. Insurance cover is needed for all staff, helpers, spectators, and property. As part of the application process, you must provide proof that you have a valid insurance policy in place.</p>
Normally your insurers will want you to have assessed all the risks and taken steps to eliminate or control as many as possible.</p>
Ensure you obtain copies of any contractor’s insurance policies and keep on file.</p>
Salford City Council does not accept any liability for any loss or damage caused to persons or property howsoever caused as a result of your event or public gathering.</p>
The event organiser shall at their own cost obtain public liability insurance which must indemnify the council against any and all claims, demands and proceedings arising out of your event.</p>
 </div>
 The Purple Guide to health, safety and welfare at outdoor events </p>
 </div>
The event manager must be able to comply with the guidance in the Health and Safety Executive document ‘ The Purple Guide ’ and must be familiar with event safety planning guidance, crowd ratios and emergency protocols as laid out in this document. </p>
For larger event it is worth the event organiser purchasing a subscription for The Purple Guide. A subscription to access the content of the Purple Guide website at www.thepurpleguide.co.uk costs £50 plus VAT for the year for a single user. There are also multi-user subscription plans available.</p>
 </div>
 Planning ahead </p>
 </div>
The application form will only be activated if your event takes place six weeks from the date of application.</p>
Events within six weeks of application will not be accepted.</p>
The application form requires details of your event management plans, so you will need to have these plans at hand when you start the form.</p>
Private barbeque and cooking fires are not permitted as part of events. Unless provided by a registered food vendor and/or after consultation with the council.</p>
 </div>
 Food and drink at events in Salford </p>
 </div>
Please note that Food Hygiene Legislation applies to any activity that involves handling food and drink. Anyone serving or selling food at an event on Salford land must adhere to current food hygiene legislation which is Regulation (EC) 852/2004 Hygiene of Foodstuffs and the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013.</p>
Food businesses also need to comply with the General Food Law Regulation (EC) 178/2002 and the General Food Regulations 2004.</p>
If you want to trade as a mobile caterer within Salford, you must either, already be registered with Salford, or apply for a temporary consent to trade. The registration process ensures that caterers or vendors adhere to current legislation. An event organiser must confirm with the caterer or vendor that they are registered with the local authority where they store their mobile vehicle or where they produce their food. If the business is not registered with their local authority they must apply. If the trader does not currently have consent to trade within Salford, they must apply for a temporary street trading licence. All this information must be provided within the event paperwork.</p>
Please note any traders that you want to attend an event must be a minimum of a 4 rating, have been inspected in the last two years. We will not accept a trader that is awaiting inspection.</p>
Please read the  downloadable Event Organisers Food and Beverage Traders Checklist at the bottom of the page.</p>
Ticketed and fenced events will be exempt from street trading charges.</p>
 View a full list of registered vendors (Adobe PDF format) . </p>
 Please carefully consider the consequences for you, your staff, volunteers, contractors, suppliers, and the public, if permission for your traders to attend is withdrawn by Salford City Council for any reason. </p>
Salford City Council will not be liable for any loss of income or costs associated with cancellation, or withdrawal of permission for traders to attend your event if their paperwork is incomplete.</p>
Salford City council officers may attend your event to inspect traders on site. No notice of their attendance may be given; however they will make the attendance known to you on their arrival along with the purpose of their visit. This provision in made under the Licensing Act 2003 .</p>

There are three steps to assessing your application</h2>
 Step one </p>
 </div>
Salford City Council will check availability of the location on the dates and times you have requested. They will check the suitability of the space for the event described and the activities you intend to undertake. Including arrangements for event car parking, and installation of temporary structures, barriers, crowd size and management, noise controls, food vending and sale of alcohol.</p>
At this stage your application could be refused if the space is not suited to the event, event content or crowd size you have described in the application.</p>
 </div>
 Step two </p>
 </div>
Your application maybe referred to the city’s Safety Advisory Group, this group is made up of key partners including Greater Manchester Police, regulatory officers, and other emergency services. You may be asked to supply your operational and site plans along with significant crowd management plans for SAG to review before a decision is made.</p>
You may be asked to attend a SAG meeting to discuss your event - this will be a virtual meeting on Microsoft Teams.</p>
You will be asked to supply significant paperwork depending on the size and activities being planned. Your must have this ready to submit. The paperwork must be in date, site specific, properly labelled and titled. File and documents that are not properly titled with content and event title will not be accepted. </p>
Your suppliers and contractors will also be required to submit their company paperwork by a deadline.</p>
This paperwork may include:</p> 
Public liability insurance</p></li>
Event management plan</p></li>
Medical plan </p></li>
Method statement</p></li>
Risk assessment</p></li>
Safety inspection documents</p></li>
Company health and safety policies</p></li>
Technical documents and diagrams</p></li>
Animal welfare documents</p></li> 
Your application may be refused at this stage if the documents requested are not received by the deadline or are ‘out of date’ or not site specific.</p>
A downloadable template for an event management plan can be found at the bottom on this page.</p>
 Sports, fitness, and wellbeing sessions </p>
Fitness instructors, coaches, and wellness activity leaders must provide evidence of their qualifications and in some cases DBS checks if working with children and vulnerable adults.</p>
 </div>
 Step three </p>
 </div>
You will then be notified of the outcome of your application, along with deadlines for any additional paperwork, licensing, site fees or insurance documents required.</p>
For some events, you will be asked to sign an Indemnity Agreement.</p>
Where fees are required, you will be given a breakdown of the fees and a deadline for payments.</p>
Should your application be refused, an explanation of the decision will be given. The final decision will be made by the Salford Safety Advisory Group in the event of an appeal.</p>
