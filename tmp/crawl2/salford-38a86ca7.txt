Japanese Knotweed</h1>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
What is it?</h2>
Japanese Knotweed (Fallopia japonica) is a fast-growing, invasive plant originally introduced to the UK from East Asia in the mid-19th century. It was admired for its ornamental appearance and ability to stabilise soil, but it quickly escaped cultivation and spread into the wild.</p>
Today, Japanese Knotweed is considered one of the most damaging invasive species in the UK. It thrives in a wide range of environments and can grow rapidly, forming dense thickets that overwhelm native plants and cause serious structural damage.</p>
You can identify Japanese Knotweed using the Non-Native Species Secretariat guide.</p>
Why it’s a problem</h2>
Japanese Knotweed:</p> 
Can grow through concrete, brick, and tarmac , damaging buildings, roads, and flood defences.</p></li>
Forms dense clusters that crowd out native plants and wildlife.</p></li>
Is extremely difficult to remove without specialist treatment.</p></li> 
Legal information</h2> 
It is not illegal to have Japanese Knotweed on your land.</p></li>
You are not required to report it or remove it.</p></li>
However, you must not :
</p> 
Allow it to spread onto neighbouring land – the affected landowner could take legal action.</p></li>
 Plant or encourage its spread outside your property – this includes moving contaminated soil or plant material.</p></li> </li> 
 You could be prosecuted if you allow Japanese Knotweed to spread from your land. </p>
</div>
What you should do</h2>
On your property</h3>
You are encouraged to control Japanese Knotweed to prevent it becoming a wider issue.</p> 
 Visit GOV.UK for advice on control and disposal .</p></li>
 Do not: 
</p> 
Put any part of the plant in your green garden waste bin, black wheelie bin, or compost.</p></li>
Take it to a tip, recycling centre, or waste transfer station.</p></li>
Dump or fly-tip cuttings.</p></li> </li> 
If you are a tenant, report Japanese Knotweed to your housing association or landlord.</p>
On neighbouring land</h3>
If you’re concerned about Japanese Knotweed on a neighbour’s property:</p> 
Try to speak with them directly – they may not be aware of the issue.</p></li>
If the problem continues, you may consider legal action. See take action through the courts on GOV.UK.</p></li> 
Near railways</h3>
If you find Japanese Knotweed near a railway line, embankment, or station, contact Network Rail to request treatment.</p>
Reporting Japanese Knotweed in Salford</h2>
If you’re concerned about Japanese Knotweed on land in Salford:</p> 
 Let us know if it’s on council-owned land – we will include it in our treatment programme. You can check which land is owned by the council on our interactive maps .</p></li>
We do not treat Japanese Knotweed on privately owned land. You’ll need to contact the landowner.</p></li>
You can find land ownership information on the GOV.UK website.</p></li>
