Consultation Overview
The consultation was open from 18 June 2026 to 25 August 2026, a period of
approximately two months, during which residents, park users and stakeholders
were invited to comment on proposals to prevent unauthorised vehicle access to
Moorside Park and Beechfield Farm. A total of 290 responses were received.
1. Do you live within a 10-minute walk of Moorside Park / Beechfield
Farm or use it frequently?
The consultation received 290 responses, with the overwhelming majority coming
from people who either live within a short walking distance of the park or use the site
regularly. This demonstrates that the feedback was provided primarily by residents,
sports club members, dog walkers, families and other regular users who have a
strong connection to the park and are directly affected by its management.
                                                             1
Of the 290 responses received:
   •   267 respondents (91.7%) answered Yes
   •   20 respondents (6.9%) answered No
   •   3 respondents (1.4%) answered Maybe
This confirms that the consultation findings are overwhelmingly representative of
those who live near the park or use it regularly.
2. Are you concerned by recent unauthorised vehicle access to
Moorside Park / Beechfield Farm?
There was near unanimous concern regarding recent unauthorised vehicle access.
Respondents described the issue as having a significant impact on the local
community, citing damage to sports pitches, disruption to organised activities,
littering, environmental damage and restrictions on the use of the park by residents.
Many respondents highlighted the impact on junior football, local sports clubs and
community events, with several stating that urgent action is required to prevent
future incidents.
Of the 290 responses received:
   •   287 respondents (99.0%) answered Yes
   •   3 respondents (1.0%) answered Maybe
   •   No respondents answered No
This demonstrates a clear and overwhelming concern amongst residents and park users
regarding the ongoing issue of unauthorised vehicle access.

3. Which issues do you think are important to balance when
limiting unauthorised vehicle access to Moorside Park?
Respondents identified several important considerations which should be balanced
when developing access restrictions.
Effectiveness of restriction measures was the most frequently identified priority.
Residents repeatedly stated that any solution must successfully prevent future
unauthorised vehicle access and avoid recurring damage to the park.
Protection of mature trees was also identified as a major concern. Many
respondents acknowledged the importance of preserving the park's established tree
stock and requested that any installation be informed by specialist arboricultural
advice.
Accessibility was raised frequently, particularly ensuring continued access for
wheelchair users, people with mobility impairments, families with pushchairs and
other legitimate park users.
Emergency access was considered important by many respondents, who wanted to
ensure that ambulances, fire services and other emergency vehicles could continue
to access facilities when required.
Parks maintenance access was another common priority, with respondents
recognising the need for maintenance teams to continue carrying out essential work
within the park.
Appearance and visual impact were also important considerations, with residents
expressing a desire for any intervention to be sympathetic to the park environment
and maintain the character of the landscape.
Whilst appearance and visual impact were identified as important considerations by
some respondents, they were generally considered secondary to the effectiveness of
the measures, protection of mature trees, accessibility requirements and emergency
access. Feedback indicated that residents wished to see a solution that protects the
                                         3
park while remaining sympathetic to the park's landscape character. Some
respondents commented on the visual impact of boulders and suggested alternative
options such as raised kerbs or fencing, although these concerns were typically
raised alongside support for measures that would effectively prevent unauthorised
vehicle access.
Overall, respondents demonstrated a strong understanding that several competing
factors need to be balanced and that the final solution should be both practical and
sensitive to the surrounding environment.
The most frequently selected priorities were:
   1. Effectiveness of restriction measures (226 responses)
   2. Emergency vehicle access (179 responses)
   3. Accessibility for legitimate users (154 responses)
   4. Protection of mature trees (138 responses)
   5. Parks maintenance access (128 responses)
   6. Appearance of restriction measures (114 responses)
These results demonstrate that residents placed the greatest emphasis on ensuring
that any measures introduced are effective in preventing unauthorised vehicle
access while continuing to support legitimate use of the park.
4. Do you support the use of boulders, height restrictor bars and
bollards to prevent unauthorised vehicle access as shown on
Drawings 1 and 2?
The consultation identified strong overall support for the proposed package of
measures.
Of the 290 responses received:
                                          4
   •   266 respondents (91.7%) supported the proposals as presented
   •   15 respondents (5.2%) supported the proposals with amendments
   •   9 respondents (3.1%) opposed the proposals
This means that 281 respondents (96.9%) either supported the proposals outright or
supported them subject to amendments.
The consultation therefore demonstrates a clear level of community support for the
proposed scheme. While some respondents expressed preferences for alternative
materials or layouts, the overall findings show strong support for the principle of
introducing physical measures to prevent unauthorised vehicle access.
Most respondents supported the introduction of boulders, bollards and height
restriction barriers, with many commenting that physical measures are necessary to
protect the park from future incursions. Several respondents stated that they
considered the proposals to be the quickest and most practical solution available.
Importantly, the consultation does not demonstrate significant community opposition
to the proposed use of boulders. While a small number of respondents objected to
their use, many residents specifically identified boulders as:
   •   An effective deterrent to unauthorised vehicle access.
   •   A practical long-term solution.
   •   A more natural and landscape-appropriate option than extensive fencing or
       urban-style barriers.
   •   A measure already proven to work successfully elsewhere.
Many respondents suggested that if boulders are used, they should be installed in a
way that protects mature trees and minimises visual impact.
A number of respondents commented specifically on the appearance of the
proposed boulders, with some suggesting that raised kerbs, fencing or alternative
forms of barrier could provide a more visually attractive solution. However, these
comments generally related to the detailed design and appearance of the scheme
rather than opposition to the objective of preventing unauthorised vehicle access.
Throughout the consultation, residents consistently identified effectiveness as the
most important factor, indicating that protecting the park from further incursions was
considered a higher priority than the visual appearance of the access control
measures.
A smaller proportion of respondents either opposed the proposals or supported them
only with amendments. The main reasons given were concerns about the potential
visual impact of large numbers of boulders, possible effects on mature tree roots and
uncertainty regarding the long-term maintenance requirements of the proposed
features.

While alternative suggestions were put forward, the principal concerns raised related
to the detailed design and placement of boulders rather than opposition to the need
for intervention. Many respondents who requested amendments nevertheless
continued to support the overall approach.
Overall, the results provide strong evidence of community support for the proposed
package of measures, including the use of boulders, bollards and height restriction
barriers.
5. Do you have any alternative suggestions that would prevent
unauthorised vehicle access without causing harm to mature trees,
parkland features or legitimate park users?
Respondents provided a wide range of alternative suggestions and additional ideas.
The most common alternative suggestions included:
   •   Raised kerbs around vulnerable access points.
   •   Knee-rail or post-and-rail fencing.
   •   Timber barriers and heavy-duty gates.
   •   Additional bollards, including retractable or lockable bollards.
   •   Perimeter fencing around key areas.
   •   Additional tree and shrub planting.
   •   CCTV and monitored cameras.
   •   Improved enforcement and quicker action against unauthorised
       encampments.
   •   Locking of existing access gates when not required for maintenance or
       emergencies.
   •   Enhanced signage and penalties for unauthorised vehicle access.
Several respondents suggested combining physical barriers with management
measures such as CCTV, enforcement and controlled gate access.
It is important to note that alternative suggestions were generally raised as possible
refinements rather than objections to intervention. Many respondents who proposed
                                           6
alternatives still indicated support for the principle of restricting unauthorised vehicle
access.
Many respondents who supported the use of boulders requested that the final design
be amended to reduce the number used or that installation should avoid sensitive
root protection areas around mature trees.
A recurring theme throughout the comments was the desire to find a solution that
both protects the natural environment and prevents recurring damage to sports
facilities, public open space and community assets.
Overall Summary
The consultation demonstrates a clear community desire to prevent future
unauthorised vehicle access to Moorside Park and Beechfield Farm.
The findings show that:
   •   91.7% of respondents live nearby or use the park regularly.
   •   99.0% of respondents are concerned about unauthorised vehicle
       access.
   •   96.9% of respondents support the proposed scheme either fully or
       subject to amendments.
Residents overwhelmingly expressed concern about recent incidents and their
impact on local people, sports clubs and the park environment.
The strongest messages received were that any solution should:
   •   Be effective at preventing vehicle access.
   •   Protect mature trees and landscape features.
   •   Maintain accessibility for legitimate users.
   •   Preserve emergency and maintenance access.
   •   Respect the appearance and character of the park.
   •   Be implemented as soon as possible.

While there are differing views on the exact design of access restrictions, the
consultation demonstrates broad support for intervention and clear backing for the
proposed scheme. The feedback indicates that concerns are primarily related to
detailed design considerations, particularly tree protection and visual appearance,
rather than opposition to the use of boulders or other physical access control
measures.
Whilst a minority of respondents raised concerns regarding the visual appearance of
boulders and suggested alternative solutions, the consultation findings show that
these views were outweighed by the strong support for measures capable of
preventing future unauthorised vehicle access. The principal concerns raised by
residents related to detailed design considerations, including visual impact and tree
protection, rather than opposition to the proposed intervention itself.
Overall, the consultation provides strong evidence that residents and park users
support the introduction of measures to prevent unauthorised vehicle access and
protect Moorside Park and Beechfield Farm for future community use.
