Our approach to weed control</h1>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
As land managers, we have a duty of care to ensure that weeds do not pose a hazard to the public. To effectively manage weeds including invasive species such as Japanese Knotweed and Giant Hogweed, we use products containing Glyphosate, a herbicide licensed for use by the EU in 2017.</p>
However, we are committed to reducing our reliance on Glyphosate and continually seek alternative solutions for weed management.</p>
Steps we are taking to reduce Glyphosate use</h2>
We are actively implementing a range of measures to minimise pesticide use while keeping public spaces safe and well-maintained:</p>
 Prioritising non-pesticide solutions – We always consider the most effective non-chemical treatment options first.</li>
 Trialling alternatives – We continuously test new and innovative weed control methods.</li>
 Integrated weed management – Where necessary, pesticides are used as part of a broader strategy that combines multiple weed control techniques.</li>
 Enhanced cleaning regime – Regular deep cleaning of central reservations, A-roads, and crossing islands removes soil build-up, preventing weed growth.</li>
 Eliminating spraying in certain areas – We no longer spray pesticides around mature trees in parks and greenspaces.</li>
 Reducing spraying on highways – The maximum number of treatments on public highways has been reduced from three to two per year, except where necessary for safety.</li>
 Expanding ‘no mow’ areas – Increasing areas of natural growth in parks and greenspaces supports biodiversity and reduces the need for weed control.</li>
Our goal is to balance effective weed management with environmental responsibility, creating safer and greener spaces for everyone.</p>
