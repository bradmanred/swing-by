Peel Park: Timeline</h1>
 </div>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
 Delve into the rich and exciting history of Peel Park- From 1846, see how the park came to be, it ups and downs, all the way up to the parks re-establishment today. </p>
1792</h2>
Lark Hill Mansion built by James Ackers</p>
1833</h2>
Government Select Committee on Public Walks</p>
1839 - 1846</h2>
Mark Philips MP and Sir Robert Peel contribute and agitate for parks in Salford and Manchester. Both will have parks named after them in 1846.</p>
7 years of intense campaigning leads Manchester & Salford to set up the Committee for Public Walks, Gardens & Playgrounds. A private subscription raises funds.</p>
1845 - 1846</h2>
Joshua Major & Son win a competition for park design.</p>
22 August 1846</h2>
Peel Park opens in former Lark Hill Mansion Estate.</p>
November 1850</h2>
Salford Museum and Art Gallery opens.</p>
1850</h2>
In response to Sir Robert Peel’s sudden death, statue of Peel installed. Start of statue collection.</p>
1851</h2>
80,000 people attend Queen Victoria’s visit to the park.</p>
1857</h2>
Statue Erected to Queen Victoria.</p>
1861</h2>
Statue erected to Prince Consort on his death.</p>
16 November 1866</h2>
River Irwell floods. Obelisk erected 1867, recording height at 8’6’’. 1876 second flood height added at 4’3’’.</p>
1896</h2>
Royal Technical College opens within the park; now the Peel Building.</p>
1915 - 1928</h2>
LS Lowry studies at Technical College. Peel park features in many of his artworks.</p>
1930</h2>
Salford Art Gallery begins its LS Lowry collection.</p>
1936 - 1937</h2>
Lark Hill Mansion demolished and replaced with new wing.</p>
Victoria Arch declared unsafe and demolished.</p>
1954</h2>
Brotherton, Cobden and Peel statues moved into storage.</p>
1954 - 1967</h2>
Major redevelopment and landscaping of park and surrounding area, with extension of Technical School becoming eventually University of Salford.</p>
1969</h2>
Brotherton and Peel statues sold to private collector.</p>
1981</h2>
Crescent Conservation Area designated: Includes Museum & Art Gallery and Peel Building.</p>
2000</h2>
Sculptures Added: 'Fabric of Nature' 'Monument to the third Millennium'.</p>
Also Marie Curie 'Field of Hope'.</p>
2008</h2>
Peel Park & David Lewis Recreation Ground added to Conservation Area.</p>
2008 - onwards</h2>
University of Salford Masterplan identifies major redevelopment around Peel Park.</p>
2013</h2>
Stage 1 HLF bid submitted.</p>
August 2016</h2>
Park works begin.</p>
13 October 2017</h2>
The park reopens with a Fire Garden event.</p>
2018</h2>
After a few months for grass and plants to establish a celebration event to be held in Spring.</p>
Peel Park Party</h2>
Walk the Plank helped deliver this community themed event, supported by the Friends of Peel Park, the local community and Salford City Council.</p>
Members of the Brotherton family made the journey up north to help celebrate the return of the Joseph Brotheron statue and attend the official opening of the park. It was a great day for the park, the local community, our funders and partners and a proud moment for the Brotherton family.</p>
Green Flag Award</h2>
Peel Park awarded Green Flag Award.</p>
