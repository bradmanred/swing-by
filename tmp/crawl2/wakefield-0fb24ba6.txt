Public Rights of Way legal information</h1>
The Definitive Map is a legal document and Wakefield Council is responsible for ensuring the information on it is accurate. New rights of way can be established over time or created. Existing paths can be diverted in certain circumstances. Changes to the Definitive Map and the network of public paths must be made by Legal Order.</p>
Current Public Rights of Way Consultations</h2>
When changes to Public Rights of Way are proposed, Wakefield Council will consult on the changes including putting notices on site. We will also seek comments when investigating applications to modify the Definitive Map.</p>

Definitive Map Modification Orders</h2>
The Council must keep the Definitive Map up to date and modify it when changes come to light. This can include where new rights of way have become established or where there is evidence the information recorded is incorrect. When a Modification Order is made, it is advertised for a period of 42 days within which time representations can be made.</p>

Public Path Orders</h2>
Public Path Orders make changes to paths that already exist or can be used to create new paths. These Orders can be made for a number of reasons. When an Order is made, it is advertised for a period of 28 days within which time representations can be made.</p>
For information on applying to change a right of way, please view our Application Forms and Fees Documents.</p>

Register of Applications to Modify the Definitive Map - Section 53 register</h2>
The Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000 states that the Council must provide a register of definitive map modification orders .</p>
Please be aware that the online register and mapping is as up-to-date as possible. It may not however include the most recent changes.</p>

Register of Landowner Declaration - S31(6)</h2>
What is a section 31(6) declaration?</h3>
If a landowner wishes to prevent a public right of way being established across their land through use by the public, then they must ensure that the potential right is challenged in some way.</p>
This can take the form of erecting gates, putting up signs or challenging people using the route (amongst other methods). Whilst these approaches can be effective they suffer some drawbacks - signs can be torn down or the wording may be insufficiently specific to form a valid challenge. </p>
To provide a more robust method of establishing the position in relation to rights of way on a landholding, a landowner can lodge documents with the local authority, as specified by section 31(6) of the Highways Act 1980. </p>
Register of Landowner Declarations</h3>
Under the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000, Wakefield Council is required to make landowner declarations available for public inspection.</p>
 View the register </p>
Section 15A of the Commons Act 2006 enables landowners to protect themselves against a parcel of land being registered as a village green or common based solely on a period of use by the public.</p>
If you are a landowner and wish to submit a statutory declaration under either the Section 31(6) of the Highways Act 1980 or Section 15A of the Commons Act 2006 please visit the GOV.UK Commons Act 2006 page for the relevant guidance and forms required.</p>
For further information please contact via phone or email.</p>
