Historical interest</h2>
In medieval times this area became part of the open farmland between Wakefield (to the east) and the "Outwood" (to the north-west). The ridge and furrow patterns in the south-west of the park show evidence of these fields.</p>
By 1845 the fields had been enclosed by hedgerows so that animals could be kept inside. The oldest hedgerow trees on the site have horizontal trunks.</p>
During the 19th century a railway line was built across the valley to link Wakefield and Dewsbury. This is no longer in use, but the course is marked by a footpath and a cycle track.</p>
Nature Conservation</h2>
The site has been designated as a local nature reserve.</p>
The meadows contain a mix of wildflowers which are best seen in late spring to early summer. Keep an eye out for butterflies such as the Orange Tip.</p>
Look above the grass and you may see a Sparrowhawk with its looping flight or a hovering Kestrel hunting. Listen out for a Tawny Owl or woodpecker.</p>
The ponds attract the odd Kingfisher as well as Grey Heron, Coot and Mallard Duck.</p>
