Newland Country Park</h1>
Wakefield Council is to create a new, large country park.</p>
It will be on the site of the Welbeck landfill site near Normanton. Over time, the Council will open up 200 hectares of derelict land for the public to use and enjoy.</p>
Newland Country Park will be a green space where people can be outside, be active and be around nature.</p>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
The Council has stated its commitment to creating a country park at Welbeck. In 2021, Cabinet approved £200,000 of funding for surveys needed before the Council can draw up plans.</p>
The Council planted more than 6,000 trees there in 2022.</p>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
 </picture>
 </div>
</div>
The site, to the east of Wakefield, is near to Normanton, Kirkthorpe, Stanley Ferry and City Fields. Around its edges are the River Calder and the Aire and Calder Navigation canal.</p>
The name of the park was chosen by members of the public in 2024. The park occupies an area historically known as Newland Park and the Newland Estate. King John of England established the estate in the 13th century, where Newland Hall was later built in the 1700s.</p>
Once an ancient township, the population of the area dwindled since the late 1800s. It was completely deserted by the 21st century. The area was a quarry before it became a landfill site in 1998.</p>
The company running the landfill site still has permission to tip waste, but restoration has been taking place for several years. Some land is already back in the hands of the Council.</p>
Newland Country Park will grow over a number of years as the company restore and release new sections.</p>
In large parts of the site there is woodland, grassland and scrub already, as well as ponds and lakes. It has the Southern Washlands Nature Reserve , a Local Wildlife Site.</p>
 </div>
 </div>
</div>
 </picture>
 </div>
</div>
It is thought the area is already home to otters. It could support important animals like great-crested newts, bats, birds, water voles and reptiles.</p>
The Council wants Newland Country Park to have a range of habitats so wildlife can flourish.</p>
Another aim is for the park to be well-connected with local communities. It could join footpaths and bike trails like the Trans-Pennine Trail and National Cycle Route.</p>
