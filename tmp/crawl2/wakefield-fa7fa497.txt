Water and fire safety</h1>
The Wakefield district is home to beautiful parks, countryside and waterways.
They are great places to visit and enjoy spending time outdoors.  They’re important for nature and they’re good for our health and wellbeing.</p>
While enjoying these spaces, it’s important to stay safe and to protect the environment.</p>
Water Safety</h2>
There are many areas of open water in our district, including rivers, canals, and lakes and ponds in our parks.  </p>
On a warm day, they can look inviting places to cool off.  And, in winter, frozen water may appear OK to walk on.  But they are never safe places to go in or on.  Even if you are a strong swimmer.  </p>
Open water is dangerous</h3>
Cold water shock</h4>
At the edges, water may seem refreshing but deeper water is very cold.  If you suddenly enter water at 15°C or below, it causes a sudden intake of breath, rapid breathing and high blood pressure. This can lead to drowning.</p>
Hidden hazards</h4>
Weeds, rocks, fallen trees, fishing lines or other rubbish may be hidden under the surface. These can cause injury or trap you.</p>
Currents</h4>
All stretches of water can have currents and eddies, even if the surface appears calm.  These can pull you off balance, carry you away or push you into hard surfaces.</p>
Unexpected depth</h4>
Water can be deeper or shallower than you think. You can easily get out of your depth or get into difficulty if you jump in.</p>
Difficult exits</h4>
Steep sides and slippery banks can make it hard to get out.</p>
Blue-green algae</h4>
Algae in the water can cause illness in humans, such as rashes, eye irritation, vomiting and diarrhoea.  It can also be fatal to dogs.</p>

What to do in an emergency</h3>
Most accidental drownings in open water happen to people that didn't mean to go into the water. They might have slipped whilst walking, running, or cycling next to it.</p>
Stay safe by:</p>
being aware of safety signs and follow the advice</li>
staying well away from the banks of the water</li>
don't try to rescue pets by following them into water - keep dogs on a lead during icy weather or when blue-green algae is present in the water</li>
 If you see someone in danger, remember “Phone, Float, Throw” </p>
 Phone 999 to get help.</p>
Tell them to stay calm and float on their back.</p>
 Throw rescue equipment to help them float until emergency services arrive</p>
Once the person has been rescued, keep them warm - take them to hospital or wait for the emergency services, even if they appear to be unharmed</p>
For more information about water safety visit West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service </p>
Fire Safety</h2>
In recent years, wildfires have caused serious damage across areas of Yorkshire.  Fires can start very easily.  </p>
But there are simple things we can do to help reduce the risk of fires in our parks and countryside.</p>
Put out cigarettes completely and never discard cigarettes or vapes on the ground.</p>
Put any litter in bins or take it away. It can be potential fuel for a fire.</p>
Clear away bottles, glasses and any broken glass. It can magnify sunlight and start a fire.</p>
Only use barbecues in authorised areas - please do not bring barbecues to any of our parks.</p>
 For more information about fire safety: West Yorkshire Fire Service .</p>
