                      Review of Equipped Play Progress
The City Council’s Cabinet approved the Review of Equipped Play Areas on the 10th July
2012. The approved report set out “the policy and standards for the provision of equipped
play areas, together with a development plan. The development plan outlines the proposed
approach for rationalising and improving equipped play areas throughout the City in order
that the spatial, catchment and quality standards set out in the policy can, as far as
possible, be met”.

This February 2019 review looks at progress to date of the development plan and actions for
financial period of 2019/20.

This review also compares the Chelmsford City Council position in relation to the UK
national environment/position for equipped play. The references for this comparison are as
follows; -

   1. Landscape & Amenity Play and Activity Latest News – December 2018
   2. APSE Performance Networks Benchmarking information per Service and Family
      group (2016/17 data).

Comparison of Chelmsford City Council versus UK National Position.
   1. The 2018/19 budget for maintenance and management is in real terms the same as
      in 2012. Adjusted for inflation there is a modest 10% reduction in budget provision
      reflecting the rationalisation of provision and improvements in the effectiveness and
      efficiency of the maintenance and management of the Chelmsford City Council
      equipped play areas.
   2. The City Council managed and maintained 127 Equipped Play locations in 2012 and
      this is now 107 locations with 2 locations now managed by local Parish Council’s.
      This rationalisation of provision has resulted in the closure of 18 play locations or a
      numerical reduction of 14%.
   3. The City Council has refurbished/ replaced 29 play locations increasing play value
      across all locations and creating 21 locations where the play value is at or above the
      70-play value bench mark (as set in the 2012 Development Plan). The level of
      investment to date is £1.470mil.
   4. The local Chelmsford City Council position in terms of spending for
      management/maintenance and investment compares well against the UK National
      picture (Landscape & Amenity Play and Activity Latest News – December 2018)
      reporting a 44% decrease in spending on play facilities.
   5. The 2017/18 APSE Performance Networks Benchmarking data shows that for
      Performance Indicator PI18a; local authority playgrounds only per 1,000 children,
      Chelmsford City Council scores 2.87 per 1,000 children against the family group
      average of 2.48. This means a score position above average and performance in 2nd
      quartile.

Performance to date.
The Chelmsford City Council’s performance is on target against the 2012 Equipped Play
Development Plan and performance compared to the wider national local authority sector is
better than sector.

Current year progress.
In 2019/20, works are in progress as flows; -

   •   Copperfield Road; the existing small infant play area is being replaced with a
       neighbourhood play area providing play opportunity for the existing and new
       communities in the area. This project is funded from S106 planning gain of £101,000.
   •   Rumsey Fields, Danbury; the existing small infant play area is being replaced with a
       small neighbourhood play area. The cost of the project is £46,000
   •   Golden Acres; The existing infant play area is being refurbished, with some new
       equipment and impact absorbing surfacing being added at a budget cost of £27,000
   •   Andrews Park; The existing neighbourhood play area is being improved with the
       addition of accessible equipment at a cost of £16,500 (funded by accessibility grant).

This means that in accordance with the 2012 Equipped Play Development Plan, all of the
Category A play areas have been completed with the exception of Crickhollow.

The Crickhollow area requires a neighbourhood facility but the current play space at
Crickhollow cannot accommodate a neighbourhood play area due to limited space and
access.

Future progress (2021/22 an 2022/23)
We are currently working through Category B. Of those the most urgent would be:-

For 2021/22; -

   •   Springfield Hall Park - £97,000.
   •   Compass Gardens/Saltcoats Park – £105,000.
   •   Churchill Rise - £38,250; There have been several requests to upgrade this play area
       over the last few years.

For 2021/22; -

   •   Rookes Crescent – £53,000; This play area is surrounded on all sides by housing and
       there are a lot of young children on the estate.
   •   Chislett Row - £64,000; The equipment on the site is spread out over a large area.
       This is the only original CCC site on the estate and there are a lot of children in the
       area. Moulsham Junior School is just up the road. The play area has been subject to
       some vandalism and its condition is poor. For example, all rubber tiles around the
       timber trim trail have been pulled up and are missing
   •   Potters Close - £33,000; CCC only have two play areas in Danbury. One is Rumsey
       Fields, which is being refurbished in 2019 but that is over a mile away and involves
       crossing the A414 (Maldon Road). The equipment is reaching its end of lifespan in
       2020.
   •   Homemead – £16,500; A small infant play area in Galleywood. There is not much
       play value and the area could be extended or relocated on the site to cater for
       children from 2-8 years of age.
   •   Melbourne Park - £116,000; Melbourne Park currently has two play areas, one for
       juniors and one for infants. This is not inclusive and we have received several
       comments about siblings not being able to play together. The equipment is also very
       dated and for the size of the catchment area and the number of schools close by, a
       new play area is warranted. Some of the existing equipment has recently been
       removed due to corrosion as it has reached the end of its lifespan.
