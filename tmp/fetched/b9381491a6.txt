        About Amber Valley
        The Borough of Amber Valley lies within the heart of Derbyshire, and has a population of
        approximately 126,200 people in 60,473 households, generally centred around the four
        main market towns of Alfreton, Belper, Heanor, and Ripley. The population has increased
        by 3.2% since 2011, with the western area predominantly rural in character, contrasting
        with the mainly residential and industrial area to the east of the borough. Children and
        young people, 0-19 years represent 20.4% of the population, with 7.9% of the population
        identifying as having a disability and approximately 2.7% of the population identifies as
        black, Asian, or of a mixed ethnic group.

 Play strategy




     We recognise that there are many ingredients for a whole-system approach to children’s
     play, and that we need to work with many different communities, services, and organisations,
     to maximise opportunities. In our ambition to deliver the outcomes for this strategy, we will
     focus our work on four strategic themes:


     Creating High-Quality Play Facilities and Spaces

     •   Consulting with communities to provide high-quality innovative and appropriate
         playgrounds, play spaces and equipment that meet local need
     •   Enhance and modify the wider public realm, community spaces and streets to encourage
         children and young people’s play
     •   Increasing inclusive play provision across the Borough.

     Supporting the Play Workforce

     •   Collaborating with a range of stakeholders that work in the ‘Play’ space and/or with
         children and young people
     •   Encouraging training and learning to build capacity in local playwork
     •   Promote the awareness and value of play across the Borough.

     Integrating Play into the Natural Environment

     •   Explore opportunities to:
         • Create innovative play spaces in woodlands and natural open spaces
         • Widen children and young people’s access and opportunity to natural play space.

     Facilitating Organised and Free Play Opportunities

     •   Work with stakeholders to encourage out-of-school/holiday, free play and play scheme
         provision
     •   Collaborate with those working in the ‘Play’ space and/or with children to develop an
         Amber Valley Play Coalition.

Current Position
Creating High-Quality Play Facilities and Spaces

The audit of play provision across the Borough will be updated to identify the current spread
of facilities, and with it, the strengths and gaps in local access to play provision. There are
currently around 130 equipped play venues across the borough, of which 99 are managed and
maintained by Amber Valley Borough Council, but there exists some level of deficiency in a
number of local communities. We will explore opportunities to improve provision in these areas
but funding, resources and available land will all influence this.

The focus for the next five years will be to improve play-facility and equipment provision,
particularly in the areas and communities that are most in need. We will look to increase
consultation with communities on the type of play facilities and equipment required, but in
the first phase of the strategy we will continue to focus on utilising available funding streams
e.g. Section 106 contributions, to improve play spaces and replace/refurbish equipment
coming towards its end-of-life. As well as this, we will look to explore available opportunities to
determine if we are able to increase play provision in areas that have been identified as having
a deficiency.

In addition to the range of equipped play areas there are a number of multi-use games areas,
and other play and youth facilities owned and maintained by Parish Councils and voluntary
organisations. Where possible we will work with those and other local play stakeholders to
support the enhancement of these facilities as part of a borough-wide plan.

     Supporting the Play Workforce

     Council teams have professional relationships across a number of services with existing
     voluntary and commercial play providers. The opportunity now presents itself, to encourage
     and develop a local coalition and connected network of providers and volunteers, to work
     as a collective to widen access to play activities and opportunities for more children and
     young people. The Borough Council, with support from Move More Amber Valley and Active
     Derbyshire, will explore opportunities to facilitate the creation of a local play coalition and
     co-design a delivery plan to widen access to play, especially for those young people most in
     need.

     Integrating Play into the Natural Environment

     Within Amber Valley Borough Council’s ownership, there is approximately 254 hectares
     of publicly accessible green space. This provides around 2 ha of freely available space
     per 1,000 population. An audit of those spaces will be undertaken over time and through
     consultation with communities, we will explore opportunities to improve natural play
     spaces for all age groups of children to encourage free play in green space and natural
     environments. With the development of the Council’s Parks and Open Spaces Strategy we
     will combine insight and resources to explore how we might integrate play into these spaces
     and, where appropriate, other provider owned natural environments.

     Facilitating Organised and Free Play Opportunities

     There are a range of providers across Amber Valley offering organised and free play
     opportunities across many communities. Linking to the role to support the Play Workforce,
     we will aim to connect current Council provision with other local voluntary and public
     providers, sharing and centralising information and resources to get best value from
     available capacity. We see the opportunity to join forces with other play providers to work as
     a collective and through common purpose, to provide initiatives, play schemes, holiday and
     out-of-school programmes together, to better serve local young people’s needs.

     For children and young people in our Borough to achieve healthy outcomes through play, we
     first need to fully understand children and young people’s needs and barriers to play. Through
     ongoing consultation and engagement, with our local partners, we will use the insight gained,
     to shape action plans and allocation of available resources to deliver improvements through
     our four strategic themes.

     design and delivery of projects, facilities and programmes. We will be inclusive and wherever
     possible, and look to ensure there is an improving provision of inclusive and accessible

     and local stakeholders.

     Actively promote a positive narrative for play

     As part of the delivery of this strategy, Amber Valley Borough Council will actively promote a
     positive narrative for children and young people’s play. As part of a whole-system approach
     we recognise that there is a challenge in raising awareness of the importance of play and
     embedding the opportunities for play in wider local authority services (planning, health,
     housing, environment, transport, community safety etc). We will encourage collaboration
     between our services, to spot and react to opportunities for the inclusion of play provision in
     local public services. This could include incorporating play in the design of landscapes public
     realm and community spaces, incorporating play into service programmes and initiatives,
     and promoting the value of play through the Council workforce. Outside of our own public
     services we will promote the value of play with our wider public, private and voluntary sector
     partners and look for opportunities to utilise partner assets and resources to create new
     play provision. In our communities and through our communication and media channels we
     will promote a positive narrative for children’s play, celebrating events, achievements and
     encouraging families and communities to engage with this strategy.

     Children and young peoples differing needs

     In our ambition to deliver high-quality play provision in Amber Valley, we recognise that not
     all young people are the same and all have different and unique needs. We will work with
     local professionals, volunteers, and experts to ensure that the facilities and services that
     we provide are conducive to the child and young person audience that we are serving. We
     recognise that play means different things to children of differing ages, genders, faiths, and
     backgrounds.

     There is research to show that children in low-income families, those who face
     discrimination, and children with disabilities and special educational needs face the greatest
     barriers to accessing free play. We will pay particular attention to this.

     Therefore, in our mission to make Amber Valley a safe, enticing, stimulating and healthy
     place for children to play, socialise and grow, we will pay attention to the needs of children
     and young people, ensuring we design the spaces, facilities and opportunities in a way
     that they can, and want to access them. This will include taking account of design, access,
     safety, support, supervision and other factors that influence how children, and young people
     engage in play.
